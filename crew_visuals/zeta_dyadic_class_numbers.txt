Odd class numbers control the dyadic zeta budget
===============================================

The real-subfield reduction in zeta_budget_at_two.txt gives two
kernels for each positive twist index. The kernel whose coefficient
has positive complex conjugation action is determined by the signs
of units in the real field. Its length is at most the degree.
The other kernel can use an archived
bound after adjoining a fourth root of unity. Together these give
a polynomial bound for the modulus-one host's contribution at 2.

Two towers with odd class number
--------------------------------

Fix distinct primes q ≡ 3 mod 8 and d ≡ 7 mod 16. Write R_m for
the real subfield of ℚ(ζ_(2^(m+2))), and put

    F_m = R_m(√qd),          F'_m = F_m(√d).

The ordinary class number of ℚ(√qd) is odd by quadratic genus
theory. Here is the role of ordinary. The narrow class group has
2-rank one, because the two prime discriminants are −q and −d.
The kernel to the ordinary class group has order two. A unit of
norm −1 is impossible since −1 is a nonsquare modulo q. The
mixed-sign generator 1+√qd gives a class in that kernel. Its
ideal norm is qd−1, and the genus character for −q evaluates to
((qd−1)/q) = (−1/q) = −1. The kernel thus contains a nonsquare.
The narrow 2-primary group is cyclic, and its order-two element
is a nonsquare only when the group has order two. The ordinary
2-primary group is therefore trivial.

There is one place over 2 in ℚ(√qd), since qd ≡ 5 mod 8. Its
completion is the unramified quadratic extension of ℚ₂. The
cyclotomic layers are totally ramified at 2, and each extension
F_(m+1)/F_m is quadratic with one ramified place and both fields
totally real. Chevalley's ambiguous class number formula makes
the group of fixed ordinary ideal classes have odd order if the
base class number is odd. A nontrivial finite 2-group acted on
by an involution has a nonidentity fixed element, by counting
orbits. The ordinary class numbers of all F_m are odd.

The same argument applies to F'_m/F_m. At q or d, inertia in
R_m(√q,√d)/R_m changes just the corresponding square root, so
it already acts nontrivially on √qd. Thus F'_m/F_m is unramified
at odd places. At 2, √d generates the same local extension as
i because −d is a square in ℚ₂. Adjoining i to the local real
cyclotomic field gives the full 2-power cyclotomic field. This
is ramified, and the unramified quadratic factor from √qd does
not change that. Therefore F'_m/F_m is ramified at just one place.
As F'_m is totally real, its ordinary class number is also odd.
In both sequences the unique dyadic place stays unique and is
totally ramified over the corresponding place of F_e for m ≥ e.

Bounding the positive-action kernel
------------------------------------

Use F = F_e and K = R_e(√−q,√−d) from the host. Let A be
ℚ₂/ℤ₂(−j), with the additional K/F character twist when j
is odd, so that complex conjugation acts on A by +1. Denote
by T_pos the finite-place kernel over F. Denote by T_pos^0
the subgroup where the class is also zero at every real place.

Represent a class of T_pos^0 by a continuous cocycle f. At
the sole dyadic decomposition group, this cocycle is a
coboundary. Subtract the same coboundary globally, so that f
is identically zero on that group. Away from 2 the coefficients
are unramified. At real places their action is trivial by the
choice of parity. The zero local classes therefore say that f
also vanishes on those inertia and real subgroups.

The image of f is finite by continuity and compactness, and
lies in the 2^r-torsion of A for some r. In a sufficiently
high layer L of the tower F_m for even j, or F'_m for odd j,
the action on this torsion is trivial:

    On G_(F_m), the cyclotomic character is ±1 modulo 2^(m+2).
    An even power removes the sign.

    On G_(F'_m), the character of √−d equals the character
    of i because √d is already in F'_m. This is exactly the
    sign of the cyclotomic character modulo 4. The extra
    twist cancels the sign for odd j.

Here G_(L) denotes the absolute Galois group of a field L.
Take m+2 ≥ r and m ≥ e. Restricting f to G_(L) now gives a
homomorphism. It kills all finite inertia and all real
conjugations. By class field theory it factors through the
ordinary class group of L. That group has odd order, so the
2-primary homomorphism is zero. The dyadic decomposition
group of F maps onto Gal(L/F), since there is one place over
the unique dyadic place. These two subgroups generate G_(F).
The normalized cocycle is consequently zero. Thus T_pos^0 = 0.

The real-place localization map now embeds T_pos in a product
of [F:ℚ] copies of H¹(C₂,A) = A[2]. Hence

    length(T_pos) ≤ [F:ℚ].

The exact size from signs of units
----------------------------------

In fact T_pos is naturally the dual over 𝔽₂ of the narrow class
group modulo squares. It is the same group for all positive j.

Let D₂ be the dyadic decomposition group of F. First note that
the fixed elements of A under G_(F) and under D₂ are the same.
For even j this follows because the cyclotomic images are the
same. Globally the intersection of F with the full 2-power
cyclotomic tower is R_e, since the added quadratic ramifies at
odd primes. At the dyadic place the added quadratic is
unramified and the cyclotomic tower is totally ramified, so the
intersection is again the real layer. For odd j the twist is
trivial on D₂, whose cyclotomic image contains −1. The fixed
elements there are exactly A[2], and G_(F) fixes those as well.

The bound by real localization above showed T_pos is killed by
2. The exact sequence for multiplication by 2 on A lifts each
class to H¹(G_(F), A[2]), a quadratic character. At odd inertia
the action on A is trivial and multiplication by 2 on A is
surjective, so the lift is zero there. At D₂ the restriction
of the lift is the boundary of a fixed element of A. This
element is fixed globally by the preceding equality. Subtract
its global boundary to choose a lift that is zero on D₂. The
same equality proves uniqueness: a global boundary that is zero
on D₂ comes from twice a global fixed element and is zero.

The resulting characters are unramified at finite places and
split at the dyadic prime, with real signs allowed. That prime
is narrowly principal. Its generator 2−ζ−ζ⁻¹ is totally
positive, where ζ is the primitive root defining R_e. In the
full cyclotomic field this product is (1−ζ)(1−ζ⁻¹), with
valuation two. The extension to the full field from R_e has
ramification index two there, so the generator has valuation
one in R_e. This stays one in F because the added quadratic is
unramified. Thus the split condition at 2 follows automatically
for characters of the narrow class group.

Since the ordinary class number is odd, the two-primary part
of the narrow class group is the quotient of the sign vectors
by the signs attained by units. Consequently

    length(T_pos) = [F:ℚ] − dimension of the unit sign group over 𝔽₂.

The negative-action kernel
---------------------------

By the real-subfield reduction, the negative-action kernel
maps onto the eigenspace in K where c acts by (−1)^(j+1).
The kernel of that map has length one. This eigenspace is
the archive's ShaMinus at coefficient prime 2. Put E = K(i).
Restriction in absolute H¹ sends ShaMinus for K into ShaMinus
for E, since it preserves local zeros and commutes with complex
conjugation. Its kernel has length at most one by
inflation–restriction for an extension of degree at most two.

E is a CM abelian field of degree at most 2[K:ℚ], embedded
in the cyclotomic field of conductor N = 2^(e+2)qd. As E
contains i, its 2-adic cyclotomic action is trivial modulo 4.
This is exactly the Procyc condition at 2 in the archive's
StickBound. Applying that estimate and the length inequality
from the real-subfield note gives, for an absolute constant C,

    log 2 · length(Sha_K(j))
      ≤ log 2 · ([F:ℚ] + 1)
        + 2C [E:ℚ]^2 (j+1) log^2(2jN).

The length here and throughout the note is over ℤ₂. The term
1 covers the possible kernel on restricting from K to E. The
class-number argument and restriction steps are paper proofs.
Lean checks the archive's estimate and its specialization from
a primitive fourth root in tools/zeta_dyadic_stick.lean. Both
report propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. The replay
used the estimate's closure of 1,205 archive modules, with 288
freshly built and 917 reused from matching archive sources.

What this does to the window budget
------------------------------------

Summing through 1 ≤ j < t gives an upper bound of order
[E:ℚ]^2 t^2 log^2(2tN) for the contribution at 2. The dyadic
scale gives [E:ℚ] ≤ 16 x^(2+8ε), and the cofinal cutoff is
t = floor(x^400). The prime-pair bounds give log N = O(x^(ε/4))
for large x, since log(qd) is at most x^(ε/5) times a polynomial
in log x. The polynomial exponent is at most 804 + 33ε/2,
which is below 805 for 0 < ε < 1/52. This estimate fits inside
half of sqrt(exp(x^ε)) eventually. An odd-prime budget with
the other half would suffice for the full cofinal input.
