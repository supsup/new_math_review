Eight readings, one parity

Give each bit weight three in its own report and weight one in the other reports. Try hiding a direct bit. Modulo two, the eight mixed reports all give the same parity, which is enough to recover one hidden bit.

Why three changes the board

Write S for the sum of the input bits. Report i equals S + 2xi. Modulo two the extra 2 disappears. Every report tells you S modulo two, and you can subtract the sum of the known bits to find the parity of the hidden ones.

In ordinary arithmetic, add all eight reports to get 10S. Divide by ten, subtract S from each report, then halve the result to recover each input. Lean checks this inverse as an identity over the rationals and proves that any seven direct bits determine the eighth from a mixed reading. Read the checked recovery.

Why weights one and two cannot give this rank drop at length eight

Eight taps on an eight-place circle fill every offset. For rank one modulo a prime, rotating any nonzero row must multiply it by a fixed nonzero number. Work first modulo two. The multiplier must be 1, so a rank-one row must have the same residue at every offset. Positive weights drawn from 1 and 2 can do that only when they are all equal, which makes the ordinary matrix singular.

For every odd prime, both weights have nonzero residues. If both occur, some adjacent weights go from 1 to 2 and some go from 2 to 1. The fixed multiplier would have to satisfy a = 2 and 2a = 1, forcing prime 3. In that case the weights must alternate all the way around. The ordinary matrix then has at most two distinct rows and is singular. Thus weight three is enough and is the smallest maximum positive integer weight at length eight for this rank drop.
