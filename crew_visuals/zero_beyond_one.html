A zero beyond one

At height exactly 200, a precise turn of the mixing dial cancels two Dirichlet L-functions at real part about 1.227. Each function on its own is nonzero there. Together they make a zero beyond the right edge of the usual critical strip.

1.227061530272348 + 200i at about 122.244614150489°

Its partner is at about −0.227061530272348 + 200i. The mirror at ½ keeps them at the same height.

Call the two phased terms A and B. The mixture is A cos τ + B sin τ. If A/B is real, the two terms point along the same direction or opposite directions. Choose tan τ = −A/B and the weighted lengths cancel. At this crossing the ratio is about 1.585, so the cancelling angle lies between 90° and 180°.

As the angle increases past the crossing, both zeros move down through height 200. The all-angle count uses height 201 so pairs can only appear or disappear by meeting on the line. The count at the single angle of 45° can use height 200.

How the crossing was certified

On the horizontal line at height 200 we check the sign of H = Im(A B̄). The sign is the same as that of Im(A/B) where B is nonzero. The derivative of H is positive just to the right of ½. Interval arithmetic bounds H above zero up to real part 1.226 and below zero from 1.228 to 4. The derivative is strictly negative between 1.226 and 1.228, giving one crossing there. A bound on the Dirichlet series covers real parts from 4 onward.

The real part is between 1.2270615302723483 and 1.2270615302723484. In that interval the ratio has a nonzero derivative, so this is a simple zero. The imaginary part of ds/dτ is about −2.12154 per radian. We used the ratio of the two phased series with their common factors removed. The graph uses rounded sample values.
