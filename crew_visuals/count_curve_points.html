From counting points to two roots

A nonsingular elliptic curve has one point at infinity that sits outside the grid. For a good prime p, let N be the grid count plus one and t = p + 1 − N. Hasse’s bound is |t| ≤ 2√p.

At 5 there are 8 points, so t = −2. The quadratic u² − t u + p is u² + 2u + 5, with roots −1 ± 2i. Both have absolute value √5. Hasse’s bound gives roots of absolute value √p at every good prime. Write u = ps and absolute value √p means the real part of s is ½. That is the elliptic-curve version of RH over the finite field.
