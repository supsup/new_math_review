Where two zeta zeros meet

At zeta’s usual setting, these two zeros lie just 0.0377 apart in height. Follow the same pair while turning the de Bruijn–Newman heat parameter below zero. Their two crossings meet near t = −0.0007112, then leave the line.

How the wave and meeting were computed

The de Bruijn–Newman family Ht has H0 proportional to the completed zeta function on the line, with its argument x equal to twice the height here. The Gaussian identity in Rodgers and Tao’s equation (15) computes the wave for negative t by averaging the initial values around each height. Rodgers and Tao’s paper

We computed the meeting by solving for zero wave height and zero slope together. Increasing the precision and the number of Gaussian quadrature nodes returned t ≈ −0.000711204733903 at height 7005.08171470549. The page heats a local polynomial fit for smooth playback. Its values were spot-checked against direct Gaussian quadrature at 20 height/time combinations. These are numerical estimates for this pair.

Near a double zero with nonzero curvature, the leading shape is a constant times [(x − x0)² − 2(t − t0)]. Before the meeting it crosses twice. At the meeting it touches zero, and afterward the nearby pair is complex so the real wave has no crossing there.

From the original gap, an isolated-pair estimate predicts −0.000710588365404. It differs from the numerical meeting time here by about 0.087% in magnitude. The browser’s approximation and the independent numerical check can be reproduced with tools/check_lehmer_heat.py and mpmath 1.3.0.
