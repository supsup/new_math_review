Four ways to split the odd-prime budget
======================================

The modulus-one host field K is made from the real cyclotomic
tower R by adjoining √−q and √−d. Its four automorphisms over R
flip either square root independently. At an odd coefficient prime
ℓ, this also splits the cohomology length in the ζ budget into four
lengths over the smaller field R.

The kernels
-----------

Let A = ℚ_ℓ/ℤ_ℓ(−j), with its inverse cyclotomic action to power j.
Write T_ℓ(F,A) for the classes in continuous H¹ of the absolute
Galois group of F that satisfy these local conditions at finite places.

    At v above ℓ, the class restricts to zero on the decomposition group.
    At every other v, it restricts to zero on the inertia group.

For this A the inertia groups away from ℓ act trivially. The second
condition says that the class comes from H¹ of the Galois group
unramified outside ℓ. Indeed, a cocycle restricts to a homomorphism
on the closed normal subgroup generated by these inertia groups,
and zero restrictions force it to vanish on the inertia groups,
their conjugates and their closure by continuity. Inflation
and restriction identify the class with one on the quotient.
The first condition is then the archive's Sha1 kernel.

In the Lean source this quotient is GS, and its denominator NS is
the closed normal subgroup generated by inertia away from the chosen
places. The local maps locFam use the decomposition groups over ℓ.
Thus T_ℓ(K,A) describes the module whose length the archive calls
shaLen. For a coefficient module twisted by an additional character,
the same two conditions on absolute H¹ define T_ℓ, even where that
character acts nontrivially on inertia.

How a sign component descends
-----------------------------

Write H = Gal(K/R). For a character χ of H with values ±1, use
χ also for its inflation to the absolute Galois group of R.
Restriction gives an isomorphism

    T_ℓ(R,A twisted by χ)  ≃  the χ-eigenspace of T_ℓ(K,A).

For the global cohomology groups, corestriction after restriction
is multiplication by [K:R]. In the other order the composite is
the sum of the four Galois actions. Since 4 is a unit in ℤ_ℓ,
restriction identifies the group over R with the H-invariants of
the group over K with its twisted action. On G_K the coefficient
twist is trivial. Invariants for the signed action are exactly
the χ-eigenspace for the original action.

The local conditions match. A decomposition group upstairs is
the intersection of the group downstairs with G_K, and the same
holds for inertia. The index of either intersection divides 4.
Restriction on each corresponding local H¹ is therefore injective
by the same corestriction identity. A class upstairs satisfies
the zero conditions exactly when its downstairs class does.
Using absolute inertia groups here also covers the primes at
which the quadratic characters ramify.

The four character projectors give a direct sum of these
eigenspaces. For the sign pair (s,t), the projector is
(1+sσ)(1+tτ)/4, where σ flips √−q and τ flips √−d. The four
lengths add up to the original shaLen, with infinite length
allowed. In the small odd-prime range of the host-field card,
each prime of R over ℓ splits completely in K. All four twists
then have the same local coefficient action at the places over ℓ.

How it refines the archive's split
---------------------------------

The archive already separates the odd lengths by complex
conjugation in LengthEqAddEigen. Complex conjugation fixes R
and flips both square roots. Its eigenvalue on (s,t) is st.
The archive calls the st = (−1)^j part Plus and the
st = (−1)^(j+1) part Minus. Each part is therefore the sum of
two of the four twisted lengths.

tools/zeta_sha_four_signs.lean checks the length identity for
the archive's actual Sha module under any two commuting field
involutions at odd ℓ. It applies the archive's single-involution
length theorem twice. The identification with the downstairs
twisted kernels is the paper argument above.

All the way to the rational field
---------------------------------

The same argument works for K over ℚ. Its group is
C_(2^e) × C₂ × C₂. At odd ℓ, extend coefficients to the
ring of integers O of a finite unramified extension of ℚ_ℓ
that contains the roots of unity of order 2^max(e,1).
Such a field is obtained by taking a finite residue field
whose multiplicative group has order divisible by 2^max(e,1)
and lifting the roots.

This scalar extension preserves lengths. It is finite free,
so it commutes with the cochains and the kernels of the local
maps. Each original simple factor ℤ_ℓ/ℓ becomes one simple
factor O/ℓ. Faithful flatness also preserves infinite length.

The full group order 2^(e+2) is a unit in O. Its character
projectors now separate every character over O. The same
global and local restriction argument descends each component
to a Dirichlet twist over ℚ. For a χ-eigenspace upstairs,
the downstairs coefficient twist is χ⁻¹, which cancels
the character action in the invariants.

Consequently each odd summand in the budget equals a sum of lengths
of rational, Dirichlet-twisted kernels, measured over O. The cofinal
budget requires uniform bounds on this sum. The group order is a
power of two, so it is invertible for these projectors precisely at
odd coefficient primes. The source handles ℓ = 2 in its own clause
in vvle_of_parts.

Sources and replay
------------------

The source at 6193bb5 defines NS and GS in
ReciprocityTheory/GaloisValuations/Global.lean, locFam in
ReciprocityTheory/Hypotheses/GalCoh.lean, and the coefficient
module, Sha and shaLen in Formalism/GaloisCohomology/Statement.lean.

The paper argument uses the inflation, restriction and
corestriction identities for continuous cohomology. See
Romyar Sharifi, Group and Galois Cohomology, §§1.8 and 2.2.
https://www.math.ucla.edu/~sharifi/groupcoh.pdf
