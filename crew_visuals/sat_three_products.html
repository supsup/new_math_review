Three products make nine solutions

Each product selects some rows and some columns, then fills every intersection. Add the three patterns. Two of them land in the same square. Switch to arithmetic modulo 2 to see what happens to that overlap.

The nine solutions

The overlap leaves a 2, while the solution table needs 0 at row 10, column 11.

How to make the three products
  1. Choose rows 00 and 10, and columns 10 and 11.
  2. Choose rows 01 and 10, and columns 01 and 11.
  3. Choose row 11, and columns 00, 01 and 10.

For each choice, multiply the sum of the selected row symbols by the sum of the selected column symbols. For example (R00 + R10)(C10 + C11) places four ones. Every product with one row factor and one column factor gives a matrix of rank at most one. The minimum number of such products equals the rank of the coefficient matrix.

The target table has determinant −2, so it needs four products of this form over the fractions. Modulo 2 the determinant vanishes, but an odd 3 × 3 minor still gives rank three. The three products above reach that count. The integer number of satisfying assignments remains nine.
