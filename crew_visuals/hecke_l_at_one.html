Never zero at one

One of the archive's published statements, HeckeLEntireAndNonzeroAtOne, read at the source. Lean has checked its proof in our build, in a file that brings 551 of the archive's files. Until now it was only a name to us. Here is what it says, and what it looks like for the rational numbers, where everything can be computed.

What it says

Take any number field K and a unitary Hecke character χ: a continuous way of attaching a complex number of size 1 to each idele of K, multiplicative, and equal to 1 on the numbers of K itself. At each prime v where χ is unramified (trivial on the local units), χ has a value at a uniformizer, χ(πv). Its L-function is the product over those primes

which converges when the real part of s is above 1. The statement says two things.

  1. Entire. Unless χ is a power of the idele norm, there is a function defined and differentiable on the whole complex plane that equals the product to the right of 1.
  2. Never zero at one. Unless χ is trivial, there is a function analytic at s = 1 that equals the product to the right of 1, and its value at 1 is not zero.

The second clause holds more than it seems to. For χ = with t ≠ 0, the product is , so the clause says : the zeta function of K has no zero on the line Re s = 1. That is the fact behind the prime ideal theorem. For χ of finite order it is Dirichlet's L(1, χ) ≠ 0 in Hecke's generality. That is what makes Chebotarev's density work for abelian extensions.

What it leaves out. It does not include the gamma factors or the functional equation; those are a separate statement, HeckeTateFunctionalEquation. For a norm power it says nothing about the pole. It gives no zero-free region beyond the line itself, and nothing about the critical strip. It is a classical theorem. (Its history, Hecke's continuation and the older argument for the line Re s = 1, we give from memory and did not read at a source.) The archive's final theorems claim far more for Dirichlet L-functions: no zero anywhere to the right of 1/2.

Over the rational numbers the unitary Hecke characters are exactly the primitive Dirichlet characters χ times a norm power , and . So the statement for ℚ says: the L-function of every primitive character other than 1 is entire, and for every real t, except for ζ at t = 0, where it has its pole. The page computes , with the Hurwitz zeta function by Euler–Maclaurin summation (60 terms and ten Bernoulli corrections). At s = 1 the poles of the Hurwitz terms cancel because the values of χ add to zero. The characters are built from generators of the units mod each prime power. For the quadratic characters at s = 1 the page compares with Dirichlet's class number formula, , where h is counted from reduced forms, as on the class-number page. tools/check_hecke_l_at_one.py runs the page's arithmetic in node and compares it with a separate Python computation.
