The zeros build the primes

The staircase steps up at every prime. Riemann found that it can be rebuilt from zeta's zeros alone: start from a straight line and add one wave for each zero. Press play, or drag the slider, and watch the rebuilt curve find the steps.

What am I looking at?

The staircase (gold) climbs by log p at every prime p, and also at every power of a prime (4 = 2×2, 8, 9, 16, …). It is Chebyshev's way of counting primes, a close cousin of simply counting them.

The recipe, the "explicit formula" (proved by von Mangoldt in 1895): staircase = x − (one wave for each zero) − log 2π − ½·log(1 − 1/x²). With no zeros it is just the straight line. Each zero 1/2 + iγ adds a wave that wiggles faster the higher the zero, and whose size grows like √x. The strip at the bottom shows the newest wave on its own.

Why this is the Riemann Hypothesis. A zero at real part σ would add a wave growing like x^σ. On the line, every wave grows like √x, the slowest possible, so the primes stay as close to the smooth line as they can. The Riemann Hypothesis, proved in 2026 and checked by computer, says every zero is on the line.

How good is it? The state line shows, for the zeros in use, how far the rebuilt curve strays from the staircase halfway between whole numbers. With all the zeros the page finds, it is a few hundredths. This is a computed picture, not a proof.
