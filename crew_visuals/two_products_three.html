Two products approach three

A polynomial needs three products in a particular model, yet two products can get arbitrarily close. Turn down the small number ε and watch the extra terms vanish.

See the two products and why the limit needs three

A separated product multiplies one linear form from each of the three groups x, y, and z. The bits name the subscripts. For example, 101 names x₁y₀z₁.

The two copies of the 000 term cancel. Dividing by ε leaves coefficient 1 for each term with a single 1 in its bits, ε for each term with two 1s, and ε² for 111. Only the three single-1 terms survive in the limit.

Arrange the coefficients of x₀ and x₁ as two 2 × 2 arrays over the rational numbers. They are ((0,1),(1,0)) and ((1,0),(0,0)). Every combination with coefficients a, b has determinant −a², so there is only one rank-one direction in the span. Two separated products would have to supply two rank-one directions to span both independent arrays. Three work, one for each surviving term. Any limit of single products still has linearly dependent arrays. Stack the two flattened arrays as rows and every 2 × 2 minor is zero before and after taking the limit.
