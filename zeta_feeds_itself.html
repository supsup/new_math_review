What am I looking at?

Two meanings of one word. A fixed point of a rule is something the rule leaves where it is. The rule on the page Where the mirror holds still is the mirror, and its fixed points are the whole centre line. Here the rule is zeta itself: put a number in, get a number out. A fixed point is a number that comes out as it went in.

The staircase. On the real line, start at a number x on the diagonal. Go straight up or down to the gold curve: that height is . Go across to the diagonal: that turns the answer into the next question. Repeat. The path winds into the point where the curve crosses the diagonal, , because the curve is not steep there: its slope is −0.5127. Each step lands on the other side of the crossing, about half as far away.

The coloured square. The same game for complex starting points, in the square from −4 to 6 across and −5 to 5 up. Teal: the start falls into the fixed point, brighter the sooner it gets close. Gold: the path leaves the region this page can follow (real part between −20 and 40, imaginary part between −50 and 50); that is not a proof that it never comes back. Dark: undecided after 60 steps. At the resolution of this picture the boundary between teal and gold is ragged, not a smooth curve. Whether it stays ragged at every scale, this page does not test.

Other fixed points. The curve crosses the diagonal again at 1.83377265…, but there its slope is −1.374, steeper than one, so that point pushes its neighbours away: try the third button. There are more far out on the negative side, near −20.13, −21.99, −24.00 and so on, each one close to an even number and each pushing away violently. Between −30 and the pole at 1, the one at −0.2959 is the only fixed point on the real line that pulls.

Why "close enough" is safe. The page stops following a path once it is within 0.05 of the fixed point. That is safe, and Lattice proved it. Everything within 0.05 of the fixed point lies in the disc of radius 0.06 about −0.30; zeta sends that whole disc into itself and shrinks every distance in it by a factor of at most 0.86; so every path that enters it converges to the fixed point. The proof is two inequalities, shown on The disk that keeps pulling in. The fixed point itself is pinned down the same way: in arithmetic that rounds outward, is positive at −0.295905005576 and negative at −0.295905005574, so the fixed point lies between those two numbers (Lattice's bracket, which I recomputed). The check this page runs before drawing is weaker, a sample: 64 points spread evenly round the circle of radius 0.05, each sent to within 0.03 of the fixed point.
