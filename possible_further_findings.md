# Possible further findings

*What the crew found that the collection does not say. Candidate feedback to the authors and maintainers of [`openai/math`](https://github.com/openai/math); nothing here has been sent yet.*

Every item below rests on a sentence in one of the cards of [`showcase.md`](showcase.md), named by family number, and on what an agent actually read or ran: "checked" means read at source or computed by us; "not reached" means we could not get at the source. Sections A to D are about the collection; section E collects the crew's own new results and ideas, which are research results rather than corrections and live in detail on their cards.

## A. Preprints: imprecisions, no outright errors found

1. **Family 100 (cylinder covering of the tetrahedron).** The displayed cover (epsilon = 1/2000, 16,000,000 cylinders) is sixteen times looser than the paper's own two corollaries allow: tau = 1/500, eta = 1/498 with 1,000,000 cylinders satisfies both jointly, with exact margins 1751/249000000 and 2959/5187500000000. No step of the proof depends on the 16-million parameters. Checked in the TeX (epsilon at most 1/2000 and the 2 epsilon^4 bound).

## B. Lean artifacts, comparator and docs

2. **Family 166 (higher-dimensional distinct distances).** No comparator challenge and no `lean/docs/166.md`, yet `OAI.lean` imports an orphan module (`OAI/Combinatorics/Distances/PolynomialIsolation.lean`, supporting lemmas only). Either a docs page should say the main theorem is not formalized, or the orphan import should go.
3. **Family 237 (honeycomb self-avoiding walk).** `CONTENTS.md` gives the main fixed-length theorem a Lean link, but `docs/237.md` links four companion papers and never the main paper; all four comparators are supporting results. Checked by reading `docs/237.md`.
4. **Families 150 and 143.** The docs titles name the unformalized stronger claim ("weak mixing" for triangular billiards; "uniform bounds for limit cycles") while the scope formalizes only ergodicity, respectively the quintic Lienard bound. `docs/205.md` is the model of how to say this plainly.
5. **Family 100.** `ComparatorChallenges/TriangularCovering.lean` quantifies "there exist delta and C" and the solution proves 14 epsilon^4, while the paper states 2 epsilon^4 for epsilon at most 1/2000. A second comparator pinning the paper's constants, or a scope sentence on the existential ones, would close the gap between what is proved and what is claimed. Checked at `TriangularCovering.lean` lines 52 to 58 and `TriangularCovering/Main.lean` line 19.
6. **Family 191 (Heilbronn).** `CONTENTS.md` says "for every sufficiently large n" with a Lean link; the comparator proves an unbounded sequence of n with a smaller exponent, as `docs/191.md` itself admits. The contents line should match the docs line.
7. **Family 156 (Borsuk in dimension nine), a low-priority scope suggestion, not a defect.** `Borsuk/Main.lean` derives `euclidean_nine_counterexample`, the headline statement in R^9; it is compiled and proved, but `BorsukNine.json` selects only `main_theorem`, so the R^9 statement is not separately compared. If the maintainers want an explicit R^9 challenge, the file and line are at hand. Hygiene on the plus side: 145 FiniteCases files, 13,170 `decide` calls and no `native_decide`.
8. **Withdrawn after Lattice's check: the "comparator trust gap".** An earlier draft of this page said the challenge definitions re-declared in each solution's `Model.lean` were guarded only by a text diff. Lattice read `leanprover/comparator`'s `Compare.lean`: it recursively follows the constants in each selected theorem's type and compares the non-target exported declarations, the redeclared definitions included. Our byte-for-byte diffs were human corroboration, not the only guard. The cards' status text has been corrected accordingly.
9. **Positive replication, for the record.** Kernel builds with permitted axioms only and firing sorry controls were done inside the crew for families 158 (70 modules), 091, 180 (8,955 jobs) and 173 (1,872 jobs); statement MATCHES readings were done for 012, 013, 071, 156, 173, 180 and 213, by two independent readers each for 156, 173 and 180. None of these is a rebuild on a second machine outside the crew.

## C. Complements the authors do not state

10. **Family 156, HELD as a numerical lead, not a result.** A numerical cover of the lines of R^4 by caps with a strict angular margin succeeds with eleven caps (covering radius 42.4 degrees, re-measured on two million fresh samples) and fails with ten (46.1 degrees, above the 45 needed). Lattice's objection stands: an optimiser plus sampled directions is not a continuum covering certificate, so b(X) <= 11 is not established, and a failed ten-cap optimisation gives no lower bound at all. Turning this into a statement needs versioned cap centres and a rigorous global bound (interval or subdivision arithmetic). The same holds for the k = 2, 3 sharpness of the label bound k(k+1)/2 until an analytic or certified cover is written. The preprint's own sentence that the exact number of pieces is undetermined remains correct.
11. **Family 181 (cycle-and-edge decompositions).** The exact value d(K_{p,t}) = s0 + ceil((pt - s0)/(2 min(p,t))) for every complete bipartite graph (two independent verifiers; the both-even case is Sotteau 1981; whether the closed form is in print is unverified) sharpens the (3/2 - o(1))n complete-bipartite lower bound the preprint cites from BM2024 section 6. The smallest graph needing more than n - 1 pieces is K_3 joined to four independent vertices, on seven vertices. Priority is Marlow's; publication status unverified.
12. **Family 354 (isoperimetric profile of the cubic three-torus).** Exact-arithmetic recomputation of the numerical appendix (24 baseline values, 16 gain bounds, 23 interval comparisons, the Taylor-remainder bounds) with the explicit phase changes at volume 4 pi/81 and 1/pi.
13. **Family 173 (Seymour).** A control that separates the hypotheses: the extremal proposition's concavity inequality fails on every sink-free oriented graph on at most five vertices but holds on the complete symmetric digraph on four or more vertices, so the proposition is false once 2-cycles are allowed and the diagonal-compatibility step is what carries the load. Exhaustive checks of all 14,348,907 oriented graphs on six vertices and all 10,460,353,203 on seven agree with the theorem.
14. **Family 180 (Barnette).** Each hypothesis pinned to the proof step that uses it, and a test set: a non-Hamiltonian cubic bipartite planar graph that is only 2-connected (26 vertices), and Hamiltonian duals of refined Eulerian triangulations on 32, 80 (with ten-sided faces, beyond every face-bounded theorem) and 128 vertices.
15. **Family 169 (e-positivity).** The counting permutations cannot respect the mirror symmetry (for the path P_3, 132 and 213 swap; proved for every path), relevant to the paper's explicit permutation count.
16. **Family 191.** A source-level audit of sections 3, 5, 6, 7 and 8 found no substantive gap; a toy F_7 census (2,968 dependent triples in a cap) motivates the paper's shift. **Family 097:** an explicit sharpness witness (the regular simplex, floor((d+1)^2/4)/d, optimal signings counted to d = 20) where the paper claims only a matching lower bound. **Family 143:** a Poincare map confirms exactly two cycles, amplitudes 0.6723 and 1.8782. These three are minor.

## E. New results and ideas the crew produced along the way

These are not corrections to the collection; they are mathematics the crew did because the cards asked questions the preprints left open. Each was reviewed inside the crew (an independent reviewer rebuilt the construction or census from the author's description with separate code before it went on a card) and none carries a publication-priority claim: whether any is already in print is unverified, and the crew would welcome pointers.

22. **An exact formula for cycle-and-edge decompositions of every complete bipartite graph (card 181, Marlow).** d(K_{p,t}) = s0 + ceil((pt - s0)/(2 min(p,t))), where s0 counts the single edges parity forces; checked on all 3,721 pairs up to 61 by 61 and by exhaustive search up to 8 by 8. The both-even case follows from Sotteau 1981. The preprint's family had asked only for a lower bound.
23. **The shape of the optimal partitions: one-short theorems (card 181, Marlow, with Fixpoint's probe for the last tail).** In the leaf classes t = p + 1 and t = p + 2 (mod p) a deficit count forces exactly one non-Hamilton cycle, and it is attained: at t = p + 1 for every prime p >= 5 by a short shift-pair construction (two quadratic cosets of offsets, one rerouted leaf per colour), which superseded the day's class-by-class proofs for primes 3 mod 4, 5 mod 8, 9 mod 16 and the deep two-adic classes; at t = p + 2 for p = 3 mod 4, 5 mod 8, 9 mod 16, 17 mod 32, 33 mod 64, 65 mod 128 (the last four computer-assisted below 2.1e6, 7.5e7, 4.65e9 and 2.3e10 with character-sum tails above) and uniformly for every depth from a size on. The 65 mod 128 tail uses the two-scalar page below.
24. **The first infinite composite family (card 181, Marlow).** The same first-odd profile for every hub count m = 2p + 1 with p an odd prime (15, 27, 35, 39, 63, 75, ...), by making the classical Walecki decomposition of K_m rainbow with an affine Anderson and Leonard starter on Z_{2p}.
25. **Two-scalar pages (Fixpoint's probe, proved by Marlow).** The missing colour of a page is forced to equal the uncovered hub, so a page is exactly a pair of strong starters with complementary sum-sets whose union is a Hamilton cycle, an object from design theory; uniform matchings cannot work for any p = 1 mod 4 (the alternating walk is trapped in a coset of the odd-order subgroup, so the coarsest phase count is exactly the two-adic part M); and two multipliers on the cosets of the odd-order subgroup with three conditions (opposite quadratic characters of lambda - 1 and mu - 1; (1 + mu)/(1 + lambda) in the subgroup; the monodromy generating it) give a page whenever they exist, which is for every p = 1 mod 4 up to 300 by replay and to 5000 by existence except the Fermat primes and six small cases explained by a linear count. Weil-bound cutoffs of about 9 M^4 were derived by a Fixpoint agent; Marlow's uniform theorem gives 90 Q^5 (under review).
26. **Steiner triple systems on 31 points (card 188, Lattice).** Fewer Pasch configurations always means a strictly higher chance that the first seven random triangle deletions land in the system, at every admissible order; the projective system is the unique least likely one; any maximiser on 31 points is Pasch-free with at least 279 mitres; around the best cyclic system every swap of up to seventeen triples creates a Pasch, the closest neighbour (a 17-triple swap, one Pasch) is unique up to symmetry, split swaps through nineteen leave at least four, no two-step move through volume nine or eleven returns to Pasch-free, two non-isomorphic one-Pasch systems are now known, and every perfect-one-factorisation double of a 15-point system has at least four Pasches with five attained (four reported attained, under review). A claimed noncyclic anti-Pasch system two moves away is under review.
27. **Kotzig factorizations with the midpoint colouring (Fixpoint's probe for Marlow's composite-hub question).** Z_9 carries exactly 27 midpoint-Kotzig factorizations, none containing a constant-step cycle, all at edit distance 14 from the step template, so the template cannot be repaired locally at 9; Z_3 x Z_3 carries 108; a 2-edge exchange between rainbow 2-factors can never preserve both colour sets, so the classical single-transposition merge of fibre cycles is unavailable under a midpoint colouring.
28. **Cross-card connections (cards 181, 188, 189, 213).** K6's rare triangle-removal ending is K_{3,3}, and the two removed triangles with the K_{3,3} form exactly the critical colouring of the cycle-against-triangle card; every way to cut K7 into cycles (the cycle spectrum) read against the triangle-removal endings; the two curves with exponent 4/3 (self-avoiding walk and critical percolation) drawn against each other; how greedy triangle removal sees a Steiner triple system through its Pasch and mitre counts, which is where the 31-point work began.
