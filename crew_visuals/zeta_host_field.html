Why a bigger field can keep the same residue degree

Put M = 2e+2. The real field R is the part of ℚ(ζM) fixed by complex conjugation. The archive’s three conditions select K = R(√−q, √−d). The two square roots make K four times the degree of R.

For an unramified p, Frobenius acts by the pth power on roots of unity and by two signs on the square roots. The residue degree in R is the first positive f with pf ≡ ±1 mod M. This f is a power of two. If f is even, its power also clears both signs. Each prime of R over p then has four primes of K above it, with the same residue field size. When f = 1, a negative sign can instead double the residue degree.

Read the exact count

Let v be the larger of the powers of two dividing p−1 and p+1, measured as exponents. Replace p by −p if needed so it is 1 modulo 4. Each squaring then adds exactly one to the valuation of the difference from 1, because the sum with 1 has valuation one. This gives f = 2max(0,e+2−v).

If p+1 < M, then v < e+2, and the two signs clear at that f. There are exactly M/f = 2v primes of K over p. Raising e doubles M and f together.

Read the three conditions from the proof

For modulus one, the archive’s IsChiField requires a cyclotomic embedding with a fixed subgroup given by a ≡ ±1 mod M, (a/q) = 1, and (a/d) = 1. The q and d here are distinct primes that are 3 modulo 4. A quadratic Gauss sum identifies each Legendre-symbol kernel with the automorphisms fixing √−q or √−d. By the Chinese remainder theorem the three conditions apply independently. This gives Galois group C2e × C2 × C2 and determines the field up to ℚ-isomorphism, even if the embedding uses a larger cyclotomic field. Read the discriminant

There are 2k−3 even characters of exact conductor 2k for each 3 ≤ k ≤ e+2. Each has four choices of quadratic character across q and d. Half the characters include each odd prime. Multiplying the conductors gives the displayed discriminant. Washington, Introduction to Cyclotomic Fields, Theorem 3.11.
