The three primes that can ramify

The archive’s modulus-one host field grows by doubling a real cyclotomic tower and adjoining two fixed square roots. Pick one of the three conductor primes and see how that growth changes its factorization.

Three numbers to read a factorization

In the host field, the ideal generated by a rational prime p is a product of g different prime ideals. Each occurs r times, and reducing modulo one of them gives a field with pf elements. The product rfg is the degree of the host field. A prime ramifies if its multiplicity r is bigger than one.

The three numbers help separate the jobs. At 2, growing the tower doubles the multiplicity. At q once the tower grows, it doubles the size exponent of the residue field. At d the number of factors rises from one to two to four, then the residue degree doubles with each later step.

Follow the local calculation

Let R be the real subfield of ℚ(ζ2e+2) and K = R(√−q, √−d). The archive requires q ≡ 3 mod 8, d ≡ 7 mod 16, and q | d + 1. These conditions already determine all three factorizations. Archive field and window conditions.

At 2. The real tower is totally ramified with degree 2e. For example, its generator 2 cos(2π/2e+2) has minimal polynomial obtained by starting with X and repeatedly substituting X²−2. For positive e the polynomial is Eisenstein at 2. The minimal polynomial of (1+√−q)/2 reduces to X²+X+1, which gives residue degree 2, and the corresponding polynomial for d reduces to X(X+1), which splits. Thus r = 2e, f = 2 and g = 2.

At q. The square root √−q supplies multiplicity 2 by the Eisenstein test. Because q | d+1 and the two odd primes are both 3 mod 4, quadratic reciprocity gives (d/q) = −1 and (q/d) = +1. The other square root therefore splits at q. The real cyclotomic residue degree is 2e because exactly 4 divides q+1.

At d. The square root √−d supplies multiplicity 2. Here (d/q) = −1, so √−q supplies an unramified residue extension of degree 2. Exactly 8 divides d+1, giving degree 2max(0,e−1) from the real tower. The finite fields combine by taking the least common multiple, so the host residue degree is 2max(1,e−1). Cyclotomic and quadratic prime decomposition.

For any small unramified odd prime p in the actual window, the extra congruence d ≡ −1 mod p already forces (p/d) = +1 by the same reciprocity calculation. The three examples above use only the pair conditions; the archive imposes further size and simultaneous congruence conditions.
