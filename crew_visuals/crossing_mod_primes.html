Why the groups disappear

A singlet is the one-dimensional direction spanned by 01 − 10. On two bits, swapping them acts as the identity minus twice the projector onto that direction. In a piece with j singlets, pick j of the n bit positions at which to use this projector term and add all such choices.

For the top–bottom cut, a basis vector in group t lies in the singlet direction at t positions. So exactly “t choose j” summands reach it. Each other position has three choices, giving “n choose t” × 3n−t directions in the group.

For the left–right cut, rearrange the four indices into the other pairing. The identity term then acts only on 00 + 11, while the singlet term acts diagonally on the four directions 00 ± 11 and 01 ± 10. A vector in group k uses a direction other than 00 + 11 at k positions. All of those must be among the j picks, so its multiplier is “n − k choose j − k”. There are “n choose k” × 3k directions in the group.

*A dash means the group has zero coefficient even over fractions. The factors shown omit signs and powers of 2, which never affect whether a coefficient vanishes in the arithmetic shown. At the prime 2 the sum and difference directions coincide. See the calculation modulo 2.
