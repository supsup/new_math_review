Why either pair works

The mixed readings leave two freely adjustable values. Draw every possible pair of those values as a dot on a square grid. A direct reading restricts the dot to a line. Any two direct readings give lines with different directions, and over a prime field they intersect in exactly one dot.

The same condition holds when rows and columns are exchanged. It says the mixed readings have two independent relations and any two missing readings can be recovered from them. Therefore losing two rows leaves exactly the same space of possible originals.

Why mod 11 is the first prime that works

Each direct reading has a direction in the two-dimensional space of possible changes. There are only p + 1 directions mod p, so at most p + 1 inputs can all work in pairs. Losing two dimensions modulo p also makes the ordinary determinant divisible by p2. The largest binary determinants at sizes 3, 4 and 6 are 2, 3 and 9, which rules out primes 2, 3 and 5. At size 8 the largest is 56 and 49 is missing from the complete list, which rules out mod 7. An identity block pads smaller matrices without changing their determinant. For mod 11 the size-9 spectrum has maximum 144 and omits 121. The displayed 10-input board has determinant 121 and passes all 2,025 choices of two missing rows and two direct readings. Ten is the minimum size.

The mod-19 board is circular. Row i reads inputs i, i + 1, i + 5 and i + 7, wrapping at 18. Among the eighteenth roots modulo 19, the polynomial 1 + t + t⁵ + t⁷ vanishes just at 13 and 16. The kernel is spanned by the corresponding geometric progressions. Their ratio 2 generates all nonzero residues mod 19, so every pair of coordinate directions is distinct. Transposition reverses the circular order.
