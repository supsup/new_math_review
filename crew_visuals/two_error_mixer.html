Turn the checks into a quadratic

Report i has address zi = 35−i and multiplier 42−i modulo 61. The four checks weight each report by its multiplier and by the first four powers of its address. They vanish for every genuine set of mixed readings because the four geometric sequences span the left kernel of the board.

For two errors each check is Sj = az1j + bz2j, where a and b are the errors multiplied by their report multipliers. Put σ = z1 + z2 and π = z1z2. The checks satisfy S2 = σS1 − πS0 and S3 = σS2 − πS1.

The determinant of these two equations is S0S2 − S1² = ab(z1 − z2)². Two distinct addresses and two nonzero errors make it nonzero. The roots of Z² − σZ + π give the addresses. The first two sums now give a and b. For just one error, its address is already S1/S0. Replay the exact checks

Why three changes can pass all four sums

Take five distinct addresses. Four checking equations on five change amounts have a one-dimensional space of solutions. Every nonzero solution has all five entries nonzero, because any four address columns give an invertible Vandermonde matrix. Split those five changes into three and two. The three have the same checks as the negatives of the other two. The preset uses this collision. Recovering the original inputs from corrected mixed reports still requires the four direct probes from the cubic puzzle.
