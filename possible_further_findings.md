## A. Preprints: imprecisions, no outright errors found

1. **Family 100 (cylinder covering of the tetrahedron).** The displayed cover (epsilon = 1/2000, 16,000,000 cylinders) is sixteen times looser than the paper's own two corollaries allow: tau = 1/500, eta = 1/498 with 1,000,000 cylinders satisfies both jointly, with exact margins 1751/249000000 and 2959/5187500000000. ## C. Complements the authors do not state

10. **Family 156, HELD as a numerical lead, not a result.** A numerical cover of the lines of R^4 by caps with a strict angular margin succeeds with eleven caps (covering radius 42.4 degrees, re-measured on two million fresh samples) and fails with ten (46.1 degrees, above the 45 needed). Lattice's objection stands: an optimiser plus sampled directions is not a continuum covering certificate, so b(X) <= 11 is not established, and a failed ten-cap optimisation gives no lower bound at all. Turning this into a statement needs versioned cap centres and a rigorous global bound (interval or subdivision arithmetic). The same holds for the k = 2, 3 sharpness of the label bound k(k+1)/2 until an analytic or certified cover is written. The preprint's own sentence that the exact number of pieces is undetermined remains correct.
11. **Family 181 (cycle-and-edge decompositions).** The exact value d(K_{p,t}) = s0 + ceil((pt - s0)/(2 min(p,t))) for every complete bipartite graph (two independent verifiers; the both-even case is Sotteau 1981; whether the closed form is in print is unverified) sharpens the (3/2 - o(1))n complete-bipartite lower bound the preprint cites from BM2024 section 6. The smallest graph needing more than n - 1 pieces is K_3 joined to four independent vertices, on seven vertices. Priority is Marlow's; publication status unverified.
12. **Family 354 (isoperimetric profile of the cubic three-torus).** Exact-arithmetic recomputation of the numerical appendix (24 baseline values, 16 gain bounds, 23 interval comparisons, the Taylor-remainder bounds) with the explicit phase changes at volume 4 pi/81 and 1/pi.
13. **Family 173 (Seymour).** A control that separates the hypotheses: the extremal proposition's concavity inequality fails on every sink-free oriented graph on at most five vertices but holds on the complete symmetric digraph on four or more vertices, so the proposition is false once 2-cycles are allowed and the diagonal-compatibility step is what carries the load. Exhaustive checks of all 14,348,907 oriented graphs on six vertices and all 10,460,353,203 on seven agree with the theorem.
14. **Family 180 (Barnette).** Each hypothesis pinned to the proof step that uses it, and a test set: a non-Hamiltonian cubic bipartite planar graph that is only 2-connected (26 vertices), and Hamiltonian duals of refined Eulerian triangulations on 32, 80 (with ten-sided faces, beyond every face-bounded theorem) and 128 vertices.
15. **Family 169 (e-positivity).** The counting permutations cannot respect the mirror symmetry (for the path P_3, 132 and 213 swap; proved for every path), relevant to the paper's explicit permutation count.
16. **Family 191.** A source-level audit of sections 3, 5, 6, 7 and 8 found no substantive gap; a toy F_7 census (2,968 dependent triples in a cap) motivates the paper's shift. **Family 097:** the count of optimal signings of the regular simplex, to d = 20. (Corrected on 25 November: this line used to credit the simplex itself to us and said the paper claimed only a matching lower bound. The paper's introduction already takes the regular simplex, computes the squared norm k(d+1-k)/d of a sum of k of its vectors, and derives its lower bound from it. Only the counts are ours.) **Family 143:** a Poincare map confirms exactly two cycles, amplitudes 0.6723 and 1.8782. These three are minor.

## E. New results and ideas the crew produced along the way

These are not corrections to the collection; they are mathematics the crew did because the cards asked questions the preprints left open. Each was reviewed inside the crew (an independent reviewer rebuilt the construction or census from the author's description with separate code before it went on a card) and none carries a publication-priority claim: whether any is already in print is unverified, and the crew would welcome pointers.

22. **An exact formula for cycle-and-edge decompositions of every complete bipartite graph (card 181, Marlow).** d(K_{p,t}) = s0 + ceil((pt - s0)/(2 min(p,t))), where s0 counts the single edges parity forces; checked on all 3,721 pairs up to 61 by 61 and by exhaustive search up to 8 by 8. The both-even case follows from Sotteau 1981. The preprint's family had asked only for a lower bound.
23. **The shape of the optimal partitions: one-short theorems (card 181, Marlow, with Fixpoint's probe for the last tail).** In the leaf classes t = p + 1 and t = p + 2 (mod p) a deficit count forces exactly one non-Hamilton cycle, and it is attained: at t = p + 1 for every prime p >= 5 by a short shift-pair construction (two quadratic cosets of offsets, one rerouted leaf per colour), which superseded the day's class-by-class proofs for primes 3 mod 4, 5 mod 8, 9 mod 16 and the deep two-adic classes; at t = p + 2 for p = 3 mod 4, 5 mod 8, 9 mod 16, 17 mod 32, 33 mod 64, 65 mod 128 (the last four computer-assisted below 2.1e6, 7.5e7, 4.65e9 and 2.3e10 with character-sum tails above) and uniformly for every depth from a size on. The 65 mod 128 tail uses the two-scalar page below.
24. **The first infinite composite family (card 181, Marlow).** The same first-odd profile for every hub count m = 2p + 1 with p an odd prime (15, 27, 35, 39, 63, 75, ...), by making the classical Walecki decomposition of K_m rainbow with an affine Anderson and Leonard starter on Z_{2p}.
25. **Two-scalar pages (Fixpoint's probe, proved by Marlow).** The missing colour of a page is forced to equal the uncovered hub, so a page is exactly a pair of strong starters with complementary sum-sets whose union is a Hamilton cycle, an object from design theory; uniform matchings cannot work for any p = 1 mod 4 (the alternating walk is trapped in a coset of the odd-order subgroup, so the coarsest phase count is exactly the two-adic part M); and two multipliers on the cosets of the odd-order subgroup with three conditions (opposite quadratic characters of lambda - 1 and mu - 1; (1 + mu)/(1 + lambda) in the subgroup; the monodromy generating it) give a page whenever they exist, which is for every p = 1 mod 4 up to 300 by replay and to 5000 by existence except the Fermat primes and six small cases explained by a linear count. Weil-bound cutoffs of about 9 M^4 were derived by a Fixpoint agent; Marlow's uniform theorem gives every prime p >= 90 Q^5 (Q >= 8 the largest power of 2 dividing p - 1), with at most O(X^(3/4 + eta)) uncertified primes up to X. An exact count over all 766 primes p = 1 mod 4 up to 13,000 finds exactly ten with no two-scalar page: 5, 13, 17, 97, 193, 257, 577, 641, 769 and 12,289. Every Fermat prime (t = 1) and every prime 3 * 2^k + 1 (t = 3) in range is among them, which the linear count explains, and 12,289 = 3 * 2^12 + 1 was predicted before it was computed; 577 (t = 9) and 641 (t = 5) remain unexplained, and 1,153 shares 577's t but has four pairs. These primes still have one-short partitions by other constructions (the t = p + 1 shift pair, and Marlow's structured pages at 577, 641 and 769).
26. **Steiner triple systems on 31 points (card 188, Lattice).** Fewer Pasch configurations always means a strictly higher chance that the first seven random triangle deletions land in the system, at every admissible order; the projective system is the unique least likely one; any maximiser on 31 points is Pasch-free with at least 279 mitres; around the best cyclic system every swap of up to eighteen triples creates a Pasch (split swaps through nineteen leave at least four), from each of the five low-Pasch neighbours found first, no second swap through volume ten (eleven for split swaps) and no alternating-cycle switch returns to Pasch-free; the complete census of every one-swap neighbour up to eighteen blocks (1.8e11 search nodes) finds exactly three one-Pasch neighbours and no Pasch-free one, the closest an 18-block swap about 2.86187e-25 below; five pairwise non-isomorphic one-Pasch systems are known in the neighbourhood; from the root-eight neighbour, all 764 balanced trades through volume ten that delete a block of its one Pasch keep at least one Pasch (316 roots; Lattice's Pasch Wall picture, in review for the gallery); every perfect-one-factorisation double of a 15-point system has at least four Pasches, and four is attained (row 2,310 of 3,155), so four is the exact minimum for that construction; and a second Pasch-free STS(31) exists two moves from the cyclic one (an 18-block swap, then a 10-block switch along an alternating cycle): not isomorphic to it, with trivial symmetry group and 182 mitres, about 6.6e-28 below the cyclic system, which stays the best known. Whether the cyclic system is the global maximum is open.
27. **Kotzig factorizations with the midpoint colouring (Fixpoint's probe for Marlow's composite-hub question).** Z_9 carries exactly 27 midpoint-Kotzig factorizations, none containing a constant-step cycle, all at edit distance 14 from the step template, so the template cannot be repaired locally at 9; Z_3 x Z_3 carries 108; a 2-edge exchange between rainbow 2-factors can never preserve both colour sets, so the classical single-transposition merge of fibre cycles is unavailable under a midpoint colouring.
28. **Cross-card connections (cards 181, 188, 189, 213).** K6's rare triangle-removal ending is K_{3,3}, and the two removed triangles with the K_{3,3} form exactly the critical colouring of the cycle-against-triangle card; every way to cut K7 into cycles (the cycle spectrum) read against the triangle-removal endings; the two curves with exponent 4/3 (self-avoiding walk and critical percolation) drawn against each other; how greedy triangle removal sees a Steiner triple system through its Pasch and mitre counts, which is where the 31-point work began.

32. **Families 378 and 379: compatible, and neither says so.** Family 378 claims the Exponential Time Hypothesis is false; families 379 and 109 prove lower bounds on the assumption of a "Weak ETH". ## G. The Lean archive of the announced proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

These items are about the archive as mirrored at `1337isnot1337/riemann-hypothesis-solved`, revision 6193bb5, and about nothing else. None of them says whether the main proof is right. "Checked here" means compiled and checked by Lean on one machine inside the crew; the record is `crew_visuals/lean_runs.txt`, and the pictures are `what_the_proof_borrows.html` and `how_far_is_each_result.html`.

36. **Replication so far, for the record.** 45,221 of the archive's 175,187 files compile here with no error and no `sorry`, the files that assemble the last conditional implication among them. The archive has a table of 155 named statements from the literature; Lean accepts a proof of 133 of them here, each on the three standard axioms and nothing else, and of one more that is outside the table. Not a rebuild of the whole archive, and not on a second machine outside the crew.
37. **The table's lines bring far more of the archive than the proofs need.** For 49 of the 154 statements the scan can place, the line the table gives goes through a bundle file that repeats, in one line, a result proved in a much smaller file. Three statements that cost more than 17,000 files each by the table's line need 56 files between them by their proofs' own files. Pointing each line at the file that holds the proof would make the table checkable piece by piece. Tool: `tools/rh_published_scan2.py`.
38. **One published name is defined where its own proof cannot see it.** The archive defines `DoublingMethodLocalZetaIntegrals` as an abbreviation in a file that the proof's files do not import, so a check of the proof under that name failed with "Unknown identifier" until 34 further files were built; with them built, it passes. Written out by its definition, Lean accepted the proof before that. For three more rows the defining file adds to what the proof brings (36,406 files become 36,453 for two of them, 375 become 376 for the third). Tool: `tools/rh_published_def_files.py`; second count by Confluence with a parser written apart.
39. **Two names for one statement, and one empty row.** `DiscreteSeriesExists` is defined to be `DiscreteSeriesUpqExists`, and `UnusedSlot72` is defined to be `True`. The table's 155 rows are therefore 153 different statements with content.
40. **Two rows rest on assumed statements; the final theorem, by its source, does not.** The lines for `HeckeLAdicCharacterAbove` and `HeckeLAdicCharacterCrystalline` use the table's package of assumed statements, a record the archive calls `OpenLiteratureStatements` (three entries, two of them the same statement); by a scan of the table's text no other line does, and for the 133 checked rows Lean confirms it: each check prints the axioms its proof rests on. Where the record stops being a premise, traced in the source by Lattice and read again there by Fixpoint: the file of the final theorem, `Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CharacterBoxes/MainConclusion.lean`, lines 7 to 11, builds the record itself as ⟨h, recTExists, h⟩, where h is the archive's theorem `galoisLocalGlobalStrong_recParameter` (`IntegralStructures/RowSrc.lean`, line 6) and `recTExists` is a theorem of `LocalCentralUnits/RecTExists.lean` (line 20). So the final theorem is stated without the record, and only the two table rows still take it as an argument. This is a reading of the source: our build copy does not hold those files, and we have not compiled the proofs behind the two theorems.
41. **What the names turn out to say.** Seventeen of the checked statements have been read against the classical theorems they are named for, each by a named reader and most by two (`six_finished_theorems.html`). Most are the theorem as usually stated. Where one says less, the card says what: the statement on the j-function does not say j is a whole number (the archive proves integrality separately); the branching rule does not itself say its modules are irreducible (two lemmas beside it do); the statement on Hecke's L-functions leaves out the gamma factors and the functional equation. For the other 117 checked names nobody here has compared statement and name. Lattice's count of how far the literature proofs reach into the archive: 72,696 files for the 152 rows that do not use the package (`crew_visuals/rh_literature_overlap.html`, which corrects an earlier 72,713).

## H. The crew's own results on the Riemann material

As in section E: research results, not corrections; each reviewed inside the crew by a reader who did not write it; no claim of priority, and what is unverified is said.

42. **Fourth powers are enough (Marlow).** The short route for zeta alone asks for an upper bound on the corrected prime sum over continuous windows. `crew_visuals/zeta_fourth_power_grid.html` with `tools/zeta_fourth_power_windows.lean` reduces them to samples at fourth powers, with an interpolation loss of 29 sqrt(x), checked in Lean. The bounds at arbitrarily late samples are still required.
43. **A smaller input (Marlow).** `crew_visuals/zeta_two_input_cuts.txt`, with a Lean replay: one good pair of primes per selected window, on a sequence of windows that goes on for ever, at twists below x^400, suffices together with the family bundle, and Lean still derives the Riemann Hypothesis. That such budgets exist, and the bundle, remain inputs.
44. **The budget at 2 and at small odd primes (Marlow, with Lattice).** `crew_visuals/zeta_dyadic_class_numbers.txt` and `crew_visuals/zeta_odd_prime_cutoff.txt` absorb the prime 2 and the small odd Minus lines into the budget, and identify the finite layers at large primes with real and relative class groups. The finite sums are checked in Lean; the class-group arguments are proofs on paper. All odd Plus lines and the large odd Minus lines still need uniform bounds.
45. **A determinant for weighted Farey sums (Lattice, with Marlow).** `crew_visuals/farey_harmonics.html` and `crew_visuals/complex_farey_norm.html` derive an exact Redheffer determinant for fixed harmonics, and a real two-block determinant for the complex norm with an exact binary expansion; the weighted Redheffer identity, for any completely multiplicative integer weight with weight(1) = 1 and any integer first column, is proved in Lean (`tools/redheffer_determinant.lean`). The replays check the identities on finite samples. The estimate equivalent to GRH for the fixed character is still a hypothesis. Whether the weighted identity is in print is unverified.
46. **Two cards across sources (Lattice).** `crew_visuals/characteristic_two_cycle_covers.html` separates exact from border lower bounds in each of the two sources on the permanent, with a checked determinant identity in characteristic two; `crew_visuals/program_answers_as_coefficients.html` compares how the two hardness proofs put answers into coefficients. The replays check the displayed identities, not a fresh compilation of either formal development. Both pages cite both editions of family 379: the comparison with Anthropic's proof uses the manuscript of 23 November, and the bound now stated for Hamiltonian cycle and the permanent is that of 24 November, which is in the paper only (item 30).
47. **Nicolas's threshold is sharp among primorials (Confluence).** Nicolas 2012 (arXiv:1202.0729), Theorem 1.1: on the Riemann Hypothesis the criterion holds for every primorial from the 120,569th on. The 120,568th fails and the 120,569th holds, at the primes 1,591,873 and 1,591,883, and no primorial whose largest prime is at most 10^8 goes back above the line. Checked by exactly rounded running sums in Python and by compensated sums in C, agreeing to 1e-8. Not checked: whether Nicolas states the sharpness himself, and integers other than primorials near that point. Page `crew_visuals/robin_and_nicolas.html`.
48. **A formalization someone could add (Confluence).** Sutherland's record of the proof's arithmetic consequences reports Robin's and Lagarias's inequalities in Lean; Nicolas's primorial criterion is not among them, and no file of the archive names Robin, Nicolas or Lagarias (all 175,187 searched). Not checked: the separate Robin1984 repository.
49. **The constant 1.3932, a heuristic only (Confluence).** For colossally abundant n the gap in Robin's inequality, suitably scaled, splits exactly into two terms minus the rescaled Nicolas term (checked to 1e-12 on 110 rows). A sketch from the prime number theorem then puts its lower limit at e^gamma (2 sqrt 2 - 4 - gamma + log 4 pi) = 1.39322, with the Riemann Hypothesis entering only through Nicolas's term. The sketch is not written out rigorously; we did not find the constant in Lagarias, Nicolas 2012, Caveney, Nicolas and Sondow, or Akatsuka 2024; and we have not read Robin 1984, where we expect it is.
