Nine solutions make a double zero

Give a solution with one 1 a weight of t, a solution with two 1s a weight of t², and so on. Add the weights. Modulo 13, this polynomial has a double root at t = 10. The rank of the matrix that counts the solutions drops by one there.

The nine solutions

G(t) = t + 5t² + 3t³

    Why twice can still mean one missing rank

    At t = 10, put t = 10 + u. The constant and linear terms are zero modulo 13. The quadratic term is 4u², followed by 3u³. The first surviving power is two, which makes this a double root. In the original variable, G(t) = 3t(t − 10)² modulo 13.

    For the matrix, use nine vertices. Draw arrows from a start vertex to four row choices, then from row choices to column choices, and from the columns back to the start. A row-to-column arrow represents a solution and has its weight tnumber of 1s. The other arrows have weight 1, and every vertex except the start has a loop of weight 1. Every contributing term of the determinant contains exactly one three-step cycle through the start, with positive sign. Its value is the weight of that solution, so the determinant equals G(t).

    The eight rows and columns away from the start give a triangular submatrix with diagonal entries 1. Its determinant is always 1, so the full matrix has rank at least eight. At a root of G, the rank is exactly eight. That says how many independent directions survive at that value. Multiplicity asks how many powers of u vanish before the polynomial starts to change.
