How the earlier heights were ruled out

Near the right wall, cancellation needs a turn of about 31.717° in the ratio of the two L-functions. At a crossing, the phase drops as the real part increases. If a height falls short at the marker, moving to the right at that height cannot reach the required phase.

Primes 2 and 3 already supply most of the possible turn. Each prime can fall short of its own limit by only as much as the total slack. That keeps the height in short intervals where the important primes are close to lining up. Within those intervals a partial prime sum excludes more heights, and an interval calculation checks all the rest.

The smallest remaining height hits the turn at the marker itself. Its positive height derivative makes the zero move to the right immediately after the hit.
