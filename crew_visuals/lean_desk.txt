What Lean said when we asked it

On 25 November 2026 Fixpoint compiled one file of the archive of the announced proof of the Riemann Hypothesis together with the 14,300 archive files it imports (see lean_runs.txt, section 3). A compiled project can be asked questions that a reader of the source can only guess at: where a name is declared, which axioms a theorem rests on, and whether the proof of one theorem really uses another. This note records the questions that the crew put to it and what Lean printed, word for word. The commands #whereis, #uses and #usedby are ours (tools/lean_desk.lean); #check and #print axioms are Lean's own.

"X uses Y" here means: Y appears in the statement of X or in its proof term, or in one of the pieces Lean itself splits off from X. It is a direct use, one step, not a chain.

The project: the mirror 1337isnot1337/riemann-hypothesis-solved at 6193bb5, Lean 4.33.1, Mathlib 0df444a360ea, the archive's own project settings; the 14,301 files that Formalism/GammaFactorNonvanishing/CritLine.lean needs, copied unchanged and built with "lake build". Each batch of questions was run as one file that imports Formalism.GammaFactorNonvanishing.CritLine. The fourth and later batches were run after a second build had added the 2,977 further files that ThirdPlaceSeries/TopPlug.lean needs, and import that file.

What the answers show, in plain words.

1. Every step of our hand trace that lies inside these files is confirmed. Lattice's steps: zeroFreeAll_of_topG uses formB; formB uses zeroFree_of_rpow_window and level_family; that uses lemmaL and windowHyp_of_rpow; lemmaL uses poleData and landau_window. Confluence's and Fixpoint's steps: topDesWin uses LinkN and LinkGLOBAL; link_gl uses link8; link8 uses link7; link7 uses linkGLOBAL7_min; that uses linkGLOBAL7_items; that uses linkCore.

2. Two questions were asked the wrong way round on purpose, to see that the command can say no: landau_window does not use lemmaL, and linkCore does not use link_gl.

3. Which "Duty". The archive defines Duty in four places (three of them inside these files) and PageWin in two. The map had settled by reading which one the path means. Lean's answer, in the first lines of the second batch: the Duty in the statement of link_gl is Formalism.L2.Top3.Duty, and the PageWin it mentions is Formalism.MainNotions.PageWin. The reading was right.

4. Each of the eleven theorems asked about rests on Lean's three standard axioms and nothing else: linkCore, link_gl, topDesWin, link8, zeroFreeAll_of_topG, level_of_tiedWin, formB, zeroFree_of_rpow_window, landau_window, lemmaL, poleData.

5. plugAll, which the first run also printed, does not use zeroFreeAll_of_topG directly; it goes through two theorems named plugWin and imprimitive.

6. From the third batch, asked by Lattice: plugAll uses plugWin, plugWin uses zeroFreeAll_of_topG, and so on down through zeroFreeOn_of_tiedWin_cyc, zeroFreeOn_of_tiedWin and level_of_tiedWin; landau_window uses landau_window_contra, which uses landau_growth_contra. And the last step reads, as Lean prints it, rh_of_every : EveryCharacter implies RiemannHypothesis. The names allChi and rhOfAll, through which Main.lean applies that step, are not among these files.

7. From the fourth batch, run after a second build that added 2,977 files (17,278 in all): the theorem where the two branches of the path meet, every_character_of_green_v_win, rests on the three standard axioms. It uses zero_free_all_of_green_v_win, which uses zeroFreeAll_of_topG and shape_closed_link_n_of_green; and the shape theorem mentions link_gl and topDesWin among 215 archive declarations. It does not use plugAll, which is a sibling.

8. From the sixth batch: where the size of m is used. Lattice found it by reading and Confluence read it again; these are the same steps as machine facts. linkGLOBAL7_items uses size_windows_of_thick_density, which uses size_window_at_five and the definition SizesAt (the one in ElevenLe.lean; five definitions carry that name, and Lean says which). size_window_at_five uses the lemmas winA, winB and winL, whose statements are printed below: from the two-sided bound SizeM they give m ≤ x^(1/2 + ε/2), x·m ≤ k and x^(1/2 − ε) ≤ m. linkCore uses the statement Win_at that carries them. Neither linkCore nor size_windows_of_thick_density mentions PageWin (a one-step check, like every "uses" here).

9. Also in the sixth batch, at Marlow's suggestion: the two compiled ends put together in one line. Lean infers its statement, Green → VWin → Nonempty (HypsAlt ...) → RiemannHypothesis, and reports the three standard axioms. (It is written with 'def' so that Lean infers the statement; Lean's remark that a proposition should be a theorem is printed with the rest.)

10. How much the path uses, from a program by Lattice (printed last, below): the two theorems at the top of the compiled left branch depend, through everything they mention and everything that mentions in turn, on 92,148 archive declarations in 5,237 of the 17,270 archive files loaded. (The 17,270 are what the program loads, CritLine's files included; the files of the two theorems themselves import 16,991 of them, and all 5,237 lie among those.) Of the two definitions named PageWin, the one in Formalism.MainNotions is among them and the other is not; three of those declarations mention it, and none takes it out of a conjunction by the explicit forms the program looks for, which makes that a lower bound and not a proof of absence. A second program, written first by Confluence, took 78 minutes on the same files and printed the same two numbers, 92,148 and 5,237. Started instead from the two ends of the compiled conditional statement (a change of two lines, by Marlow), the same program finds 96,538 declarations in 5,701 of the 17,278 files compiled.

None of this is a check of the whole proof. After the second build these files are about one tenth of the archive. The theorems above are conditional: their hypotheses are stated with definitions that are inside these files, and the proofs that those hypotheses hold are in files outside them.
