The seven-tap mixer notes

Six checks, two kinds of damage

A missing reading comes with an address. To repair a wrong reading, the decoder must also find its address. On this mixer, one missing reading costs one check and one wrong reading costs two. Any combination that costs at most six is repairable.

    M = find a missing value. W = locate a wrong reading or find its correction. A dashed border marks checks the mixer would need in addition to the six it has.

    How the decoder spends its checks

    The six checks are weighted sums modulo 110,909. All six vanish for clean mixed readings. Each damaged report leaves a geometric sequence in the checks. Its ratio is the address and its scale records how far the value changed.

    First, use a polynomial whose roots are the known missing addresses to cancel their contributions. With r holes, that leaves 6 − r filtered checks. Find the unknown addresses and changes from those checks. Then use the original checks to fill the known holes. Replay the exact arithmetic.

    Why the limit is sharp

    Two valid sets of readings can differ in seven positions. In the example, three of those positions are missing. Split the other four between the two possible originals. The received values are then two changes away from either original, so the same six checks cannot identify which was sent.
