What the proof borrows

The archive of the announced proof of the Riemann Hypothesis has a table of 155 named statements from the literature, the results the proof borrows, and sets out to prove each of them again in Lean. One tile for each row of the table, smallest proof first. Green means Lean has checked the proof on our own machine, with nothing assumed. A gold frame means one of us has also compared the statement with the classical theorem and written that up as a card.

How the wall filled

Each step is one build on one laptop: more of the archive's files compiled, and then Lean asked, statement by statement, whether it accepts the proof.

    What a tile means, and what it does not

    Green means this: with that part of the archive compiled here, Lean accepts a proof of the statement under its published name, and reports that the proof rests on its three standard axioms and nothing else. For most statements the proof checked is the archive's own line for it; for some it is the theorem that line leads to. How far is each published result? says exactly which, and how the sizes were counted.

    A gold frame means the page Six finished theorems has a card for the statement: a member of the crew opened it and compared it with the classical theorem its name suggests, what it says and what it leaves out. For every other green tile that page records no such comparison, and the tile vouches only for the name; a name is what the archive calls a statement, not what the statement says.

    Red tiles are not failures. The line the table gives for each of them uses one of the statements the archive takes from the literature without proof, so we have no check of that row that assumes nothing. The empty slot is a row whose statement is defined to be "True". That leaves 152 rows whose line uses none of the assumed statements. That count is from a scan of the table's text; Lean has confirmed it for the green rows and has not yet been asked about the grey ones. Two of the 152, DiscreteSeriesExists and DiscreteSeriesUpqExists, are one statement under two names: the archive defines the first to be the second.

    None of this says whether the main proof is right. These are its ingredients, and old ones. One more statement, FinitenessOfHermitianClasses, is proved in the archive and checked here and read (card 6), but is not in the table, so it has no tile.
