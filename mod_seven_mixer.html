One signal unlocks the mix

The input signals are hidden notes numbered 0 to 6. Each mixed reading adds the notes wired to it and keeps the remainder after division by 7. Pick any input to hear directly and the decoder can recover every note, even with one mixed reading missing.

Why any input works

The mixed readings share a single linear relation in mod 7. Its coefficients are all nonzero, so any missing reading follows from the others. The seven possible originals differ by multiples of a single vector whose entries are also all nonzero. One direct reading tells you which multiple to use.

The mixer keeps all information in ordinary integer arithmetic. The seven-way ambiguity appears when the readings wrap at 7.

Five streams or six?

For the six-input board, five mixed streams suffice if the receiver already has any one original. Send all six mixed streams when you want the receiver to tolerate losing one of them. Try dropping a different reading and choosing a different direct input.

This saves a transmitted stream when the receiver already has one original. A standalone lossless file must also encode the information in that original.

Why the larger mixers need exactly 2n + 3 connections

We require an n by n matrix of zeros and ones that is invertible over the rationals and singular mod 7. We also require recovery from any n − 1 mixed readings and any one direct input. The exhibit starts with the 16-connection twins at size 6. The construction and lower bound below give the exact minimum at every size n ≥ 7.

Build sizes 7 and 8. Begin with two stars. Use center inputs a and b and leaf inputs u₁, u₂ and v₁, v₂, v₃. Mix each leaf with its own center, and add two readings u₁ + u₂ + v₁ and a + v₁ + v₂ + v₃. Eliminating the leaves leaves rows (−2, −1) and (1, −3), with determinant 7. The invisible input change has centers (a,b) = (1,5) modulo 7 and each leaf changes by the negative of its center. For the relation among readings, give the last two rows weights 1 and 2 and each leaf row the negative of the total weight assigned to its leaf by those two rows. All weights are nonzero mod 7. At size 8, split the shared leaf v₁ into two leaves of b, one for each of the last two readings. The reduced rows stay the same and the two new leaf weights are −1 and −2.

Grow by two. In any one reading, replace an input x by a fresh input z₂. Add readings x + z₁ and z₁ + z₂, where z₁ is also new. Elimination restores the original coefficient of x and preserves the determinant up to sign. In the invisible input change, the two new entries are −x and x. In the relation among readings, the two new rows take the original row's coefficient and its negative, respectively. This adds four connections per two inputs while preserving both recovery properties.

Rule out 2n + 2. Every input entry in the invisible change must be nonzero, since any direct reading can resolve it. Thus every row needs at least two ones. If there are at most two extra ones, at most two rows have more than two. The two-input rows form a graph on the inputs. An odd cycle would force its inputs to change by zero mod 7. An even cycle would give a rational dependence among the rows. The graph is a forest, so it has fewer than n edges. Eliminating its leaf variables leaves one column per component and one or two rows. With one remaining row the determinant has magnitude at most 4. With two, each has absolute coefficient sum at most 3. A coefficient of magnitude 3 makes the determinant a multiple of 3 with magnitude at most 9. Otherwise both rows have Euclidean norm at most √5, and the determinant has magnitude at most 5. None can give a nonzero multiple of 7.
