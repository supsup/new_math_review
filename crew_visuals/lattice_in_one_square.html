How an infinite lattice fits in one square

Every lattice in this example contains the integer grid. It may also contain some fractional points. Pick a fractional point to add. All its integer translates and all sums of the chosen points must come along too. The repeating pattern is determined by one square.

How this appears in the Hermitian proof

The archive starts with a Hermitian form over a number field and a family of lattices whose duals are a fixed multiple of the original lattices. A short-vector argument and induction find an orthogonal basis of the surrounding vector space made of lattice vectors, with self-pairings from a fixed finite list. In those coordinates the lattice contains the integral grid. A common denominator puts it inside a fixed finer grid. The quotient between those grids is finite, just as above. For each possible lattice, the markings also have finitely many residues modulo the level.

The short-vector argument allows indefinite forms. It takes absolute values of diagonal coefficients when bounding vectors. If a short vector has self-pairing zero, the proof pairs it with a second vector and adjusts that second vector to have one of finitely many nonzero lengths.
