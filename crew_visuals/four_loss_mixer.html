    Where the polynomial comes from

    Report i adds inputs at offsets 0, 1, 4, 51, and 52 from i, wrapping around at 60. The wiring polynomial is 1 + T + T4 + T51 + T52. Among the 60th roots of unity modulo 61 it vanishes at exactly 42, 6, 27, and 30. They form the progression 42 · 35k for k from 0 through 3.

    Each root λ gives a sequence λj that reads as zero in every report. Together they give all changes that leave the reports fixed. From any one input vector a consistent with the visible reports, the general solution is xj = aj + 42jP(35j) for an arbitrary cubic or lower-degree polynomial P. The exercise uses a fixed a and hides a cubic. Each direct reading subtracts aj and divides by 42j to expose one point.

    The transpose is the same rotating board with the circle reversed. Its dependency patterns therefore also have the four-point property. If the surviving reports were dependent, their dependency would extend to a full-board dependency with zeros at the four erased positions. Four zero values force the dependency polynomial to be zero. The survivors have full row rank, so erasing any four rows keeps exactly the same four-dimensional ambiguity.

    Why five wires is the minimum

    A report with four or fewer wires would say that the corresponding direct coordinates sum to zero on every input change that leaves the reports fixed. Yet any four direct coordinates can vary independently in that four-dimensional space. Any smaller selection can be completed to four, so the same independence applies. This forces at least five wires for every report, even without the rotating pattern.

    A smaller example modulo 29 with 28 inputs tolerates three lost reports, using five wires per report and a quadratic. Those five wires are optimal among rotating boards at that modulus and size.
