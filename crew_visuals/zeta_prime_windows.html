One smoothed sum over prime powers

Marlow isolated the last analytic step of the Riemann Hypothesis argument. It asks for an upper bound on one corrected sum over prime powers, across whole intervals that can start arbitrarily far out.

An interval calculation gives G(y) < √y throughout the five displayed windows. The analytic criterion asks for arbitrarily large windows [Y, Y3/2] on which G(y) ≤ yθ, for each θ strictly between ½ and 1. Replay the finite checks.

Give each prime power a ramp

A prime power n = pk normally contributes log p as soon as y reaches it. In the smoothed sum it contributes zero at that moment, then grows linearly with y−n. The formula removes the main growth of the resulting sum. The constant γ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant.

R(y) = Σpk≤y log p · (y/pk − 1),   G(y) = R(y) − y log y + (1 + γ)y.

The archive’s zero-free theorem turns the window bound into a zero-free half-plane to the right of θ. Taking each θ above ½ reaches the right half of the critical strip, and the functional equation takes care of the left half. Why the finite checks cover the gaps

The ramp sum is convex, since it adds ramps with positive coefficients. On an interval [a, b] between plotted samples its graph lies below the line joining the endpoints. After subtracting y log y and √y, the smooth correction has second derivative at least −1/a. It can rise at most (b−a)²/(8a) above its own endpoint line. Add that allowance to the larger endpoint excess to bound the whole interval.

How the classical RH estimate gives these windows

Let ψ(x) count prime powers with weight log p, and write E(x) = ψ(x) − x. Integrating each step in ψ gives R(y) = y ∫1y ψ(x)/x² dx. The classical estimate under RH gives E(x) = O(√x log² x). Schoenfeld, Theorem 10, equation (6.2).

For real s > 1, the integral of E(x)x−s−1 from 1 to infinity equals (−ζ′/ζ)(s)/s − 1/(s−1). The constant term as s approaches 1 is −1−γ, by the Laurent expansion. The RH estimate justifies taking that limit inside the integral. Zeta’s Laurent series.

Substituting that constant gives G(y) = −y ∫y∞ E(x)/x² dx = O(√y log² y). For every θ > ½ this is eventually below yθ. Together with the Lean-checked implication, that makes the window condition another formulation of RH.
