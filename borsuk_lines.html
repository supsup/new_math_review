Borsuk's nine: the lines of R^4

A line through the origin of R^4 is a unit vector u up to sign, and becomes the projector uu^T; with the Frobenius metric ||uu^T - vv^T||^2 = 2 - 2(u.v)^2, so two lines are sqrt 2 apart exactly when they are orthogonal. The collection (card 156) claims these projectors, a set in a 9-dimensional space, cannot be cut into 10 pieces of diameter below sqrt 2, which would make Borsuk's conjecture fail in dimension 9. Each dot is a line, drawn by stereographic projection of the hemisphere u_4 >= 0 (choose the sign with u_4 >= 0, send u to (u_1, u_2, u_3) / (1 + u_4)): the lines fill a ball, and opposite points of its boundary sphere are the same line. A slow 4D rotation turns all the lines and caps together, so the pieces flow across that boundary. Dots are coloured by the nearest cap centre (angle between lines = arccos |u.v|); a cap of radius below 45 deg has diameter below sqrt 2. This is the collection's claim and our numerical lead, not a proof: the 11 centres (from our skeptic's optimiser) cover a 200,000-line sample within about 42 deg, and our best 10 centres leave sample lines beyond 45 deg, but sampling is not a covering certificate and 10 failed caps do not rule out 10 other pieces.
