The zeta budget at 2 over the real half of the host
===================================================

The earlier note splits the odd-prime budget by field characters.
At 2 the same projectors would require division by 2. For the
modulus-one host there is a different reduction. The length at 2
is at most the sum of two lengths over its maximal real subfield,
with the second length taken after a quadratic coefficient twist.

Why the real subfield works
--------------------------

Write R for the real 2-power cyclotomic field and K for
R(√−q, √−d), where q and d are the distinct odd primes in the
window. Put F = R(√qd). This is the maximal real subfield of K.
The nonidentity element of Gal(K/F) flips both square roots.

The extension K/F is unramified at all finite places. At an odd
prime away from q and d both quadratic factors are unramified.
At q or d the inertia in K/R flips only the corresponding root.
It acts nontrivially on √qd, so the relative inertia in K/F is
trivial. At 2, the congruence d ≡ 7 mod 16 gives −d ≡ 1 mod 8.
Thus √−d is already in ℚ₂. Adjoining it to F gives K, so every
place over 2 splits in K/F.

This is the point of using F. Away from 2 the absolute inertia
groups of the two fields are the same. Over 2 their absolute
decomposition groups are the same. These are precisely the
groups used in the two local conditions of the budget.

Restriction and the one-dimensional defect
------------------------------------------

Fix a positive twist index j and put A = ℚ₂/ℤ₂(−j). For any
field and coefficient module, let T be the subgroup of absolute
continuous H¹ whose restrictions to inertia away from 2 and
to decomposition groups over 2 are zero. Impose these conditions
at finite places only. Over K this is the archive's Sha kernel.

Let M = T(K,A), let c be complex conjugation, and put
M⁺ = ker(1−c), M⁻ = ker(1+c). Over F use T⁺ = T(F,A) and
T⁻ = T(F,A twisted by χ), where χ is the character of K/F.
The twist is trivial on the absolute group of K. It changes
invariants for the quotient group into the minus eigenspace.

Write C = A fixed by the absolute group of K. It is a nonzero
finite cyclic 2-group. The 2-torsion is fixed because the
cyclotomic units are odd. The cyclotomic image of a number
field has finite index in ℤ₂ˣ, and raising it to a fixed
positive power still gives a nontrivial image. An element
acting by a scalar different from 1 bounds the order of C.

For either coefficient choice let C carry the corresponding
action of the quadratic quotient. It acts by +1 or −1. The
two-periodic resolution of C₂ has maps c−1 and 1+c. In either
case H¹(C₂,C) and H²(C₂,C) have order 2. They are C[2] and
C/2C in one order or the other.

The five-term restriction sequence now gives, for either sign,

    0 → H¹(C₂,C) → T± → M± → H²(C₂,C) → H²(G_F,A±),

exact through H²(C₂,C). To see why the local conditions carry through,
take a global lift of a class in M±. Each inertia group away
from 2 and each decomposition group over 2 is already a group
upstairs, so its restriction is zero. The same reasoning puts
every inflated H¹(C₂,C) class in T±. The cokernel of restriction
therefore has length at most one, and the kernel has length one.
It follows that length(M±) ≤ length(T±). When the lengths are
finite, the difference is either zero or one.

For one sign the difference is exactly one. A real embedding
of F gives a complex conjugation of order two in G_F lifting
the nonidentity element of C₂. Pick the coefficient choice on
which this conjugation acts by −1. This is A when j is odd and
the twisted A when j is even. Restricting inflation in H² back
to that subgroup of order two gives the map from C[2] to A[2].
It is injective, so the last arrow in the display is injective.
For this sign T± maps onto M± with kernel of length one.

Adding the two bounds
---------------------

On M, the map 1−c has kernel M⁺ and image contained in M⁻.
Additivity of module length in an exact sequence gives

    length(M) + 1 ≤ length(M⁺) + length(M⁻) + 1
                  ≤ length(T⁺) + length(T⁻).

This inequality works even for infinite length. Summing over
the positive twist indices j < t bounds the dyadic term of the
archive's VVle by the sum of the two downstairs budgets, with
the common multiplier log 2. The fields downstairs have half
the degree. Uniform estimates for these two kernels remain
the arithmetic input.
