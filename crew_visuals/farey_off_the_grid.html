Which number matters for RH?

Take each offset’s absolute value and add them. A move left and a move right both count. The Franel–Landau criterion says that the Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to this sum being O(N1/2 + ε) for every ε > 0. For every exponent just above one half there must be a fixed multiplier bounding the total for all sufficiently large N. The chart lets you inspect individual finite values. Write the list in increasing order as x₀ = 0, x₁, …, xₘ = 1. Its grid points are 0, 1/m, …, m/m. For xᵢ = a/b the offset is (am − ib)/(bm). The endpoints have offset zero. Starting the index at 1 and using the number of points including both endpoints as the denominator changes the total absolute sum by at most one, so the growth criterion is the same.

At N = 4 the five interior fractions are 1/4, 1/3, 1/2, 2/3 and 3/4. Their partners are 1/6, 2/6, 3/6, 4/6 and 5/6. Only the first and last interior points move. Each moves 1/12, so the total is 1/6.

The signed version of Landau’s question

For each position x, write C(x) for the running count and A for its final value. The plotted discrepancy is D(x) = C(x) − Ax + ½. For a fixed Dirichlet character, GRH for its own L-function is equivalent to either the mean absolute discrepancy or its root mean square being Oχ,ε(N1/2 + ε) for every ε > 0. The argument below adapts Franel’s floor sum to weighted denominators. Franel’s original floor sum · Garg and Maji, equation (4.22).

Connect the two pictures when every fraction counts

For the m positive fractions xᵢ in the first chart, the centered running count just before and after xᵢ is i − mxᵢ ± ½. Integrate its square between jumps using the slope −m. At each fraction the difference of the cubes of those two values equals 3(i − mxᵢ)² + ¼. Adding them and dividing by 3m gives ∫01 D(x)² dx = 1/12 + m ∑ᵢ (xᵢ − i/m)². This relates the squared running discrepancy directly to the squared rank offsets upstairs.

Write the count as a sum of small waves

Start with all numerators up to qx. Möbius inversion removes those that share a factor with denominator q. Group the resulting sum by j = q/d to get C(x) = ∑j≤N aj⌊jx⌋, with aj = χ(j)Mχ(⌊N/j⌋). Their sum is 1, by multiplicativity and the sum of μ over divisors. Since A = ∑jaj, the centered discrepancy is −∑aj({jx} − ½). The braces keep only the fractional part.

Every wave with j > 1 cancels against the first sine wave. The j = 1 term gives Mχ(N) = 2π ∫01 D(x) sin(2πx) dx. This bounds |Mχ(N)| by 2π times the mean absolute discrepancy. Partial summation then gives the zero-free half-plane, as on the circle page.

Bound the whole discrepancy from the Möbius bound

The integral of the product of waves j and k is gcd(j,k)²/(12jk). Divide out their common period. For coprime frequencies a, b, divide the interval into ab pieces and write x = (ℓ + t)/(ab). For fixed t, the two residues of ℓ are independent by the Chinese remainder theorem. The average product is (t−½)²/(ab), whose integral is 1/(12ab).

Under the relevant hypothesis, |aj| is at most a constant times (N/j)1/2+ε. Expand the squared integral and use the product formula for the waves. On writing j = ga, k = gb, the resulting bound is a constant times N1+2ε times the convergent sum of g−1−2ε(ab)−3/2−ε. This proves the root mean square bound. The mean absolute value is at most the root mean square, which completes both directions. Absolute values give the same argument for complex characters.
