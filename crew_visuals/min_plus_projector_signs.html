Group by three signs

Rotate the finite sample so every line has all four coordinates nonzero. You can do this with a tiny rotation because each zero coordinate can be avoided and there are only finitely many to avoid. Choose the direction on each line whose fourth coordinate is positive. The signs of the other three coordinates give eight possible groups.

From groups to a distance table

For each group, add the distance from the row point to the group and the distance from the column point to the same group. The true distance is at most this sum plus the group’s diameter, by taking a detour through the group. If the row point is in the group, its term vanishes and the sum is at most the true distance.

Keep the smallest sum across all groups. The group containing the row ensures the result stays below the true distance. The largest group diameter bounds how far below it can fall. Eight terms therefore suffice to beat the full diameter on every finite sample.
