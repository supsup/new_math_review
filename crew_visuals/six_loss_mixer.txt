A quintic recovers six missing readings

At prime 110909, take 110908 inputs arranged on a circle. Each mixed
reading adds seven inputs. Their offsets from the reading are

    0, 1583, 55273, 58490, 73564, 94050, 94848.

The mixer is invertible over the rationals and has rank 110902 modulo
110909. From any six missing mixed readings and any six direct input
observations, all the original inputs can be recovered. Seven connections
per reading attain the lower bound. A nonzero row of six or fewer entries
would impose a relation among six or fewer coordinates that must be free
to vary on the kernel.

Why the exponential gives the taps

Take E(t) = sum of t^j/j! for j from 0 through 7. Modulo 110909 it splits
as the product of (1 + x_i t) for these seven distinct addresses.

    110822, 11495, 36194, 13987, 29685, 58083, 72462.

The logarithm agrees with t through degree seven. In the product form,
coefficient k of the logarithm is (−1)^(k−1) times the sum of the k-th
powers of the addresses, divided by k. The power sums from 2 through 7
therefore vanish. Take the base-2 logarithms of the addresses and subtract
the smallest exponent to get the offsets above. Subtracting the exponent
rotates the taps and keeps the modular root set unchanged.

Why this gives recovery

The six roots of the wiring polynomial among the nonzero residues are
exactly 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 and 128. The right kernel is spanned by the six
geometric sequences with those ratios. Every invisible change at input j
therefore has the form 4^j P(2^j), with P of degree at most five. The
addresses 2^j are all distinct. Any six direct inputs determine P, after
subtracting a consistent base and dividing by the corresponding powers
of 4. Reversing the circular order gives the left kernel. A relation
among the surviving rows would give a left-kernel vector zero at all
six missing positions, which forces its polynomial to vanish.

A common rational factor of the integer wiring polynomial and
T^110908 − 1 would lift to a monic integral factor. Reducing it modulo
an auxiliary prime would then leave a nonconstant common factor. The
replay finds gcd 1 at two auxiliary primes, establishing ordinary
invertibility without constructing the dense matrix.

Replay with python-flint installed

    python3 -B tools/check_seven_tap_mixer.py

This is an exact witness at the stated prime and board size.

When three readings are wrong and their locations are unknown

Each check uses one of the six left-kernel rows. A clean set of readings
gives six zeros. An error of size e at index i contributes w times the
first six powers of its address x, where x = 2^(−i) and w = x² e.

Think of the checks as a short sequence made by adding up to three
geometric sequences. The geometric ratios are the addresses we need.
If a polynomial Q vanishes at the addresses, applying its coefficients to
successive checks gives zero. For example, with one error Q(T) = T − x,
and the recurrence just says the next check is x times the current check.
With three errors, a monic cubic gives three linear equations for its
three unknown coefficients. Its roots give the three addresses. Once we
know the addresses, the first three checks give the three weights.

For the exact count, form the 1×1, 2×2 and 3×3 matrices whose (i,j)-entry
is check i+j, with indices starting at zero. When there are t errors,
the order-t matrix is V diag(w) Vᵀ, where column j of V consists of the
first t powers of address j. Distinct addresses and nonzero weights make
this matrix invertible. Each larger matrix has rank at most t. So choose
the largest invertible matrix, even when the earliest checks are zero.

This repairs the mixed readings. Six direct input values still determine
the part of the original input erased by the modular mixer. Three is the
sharp guarantee for unknown output errors. The replay also shows four
wrong readings with the same checks as a different set of three. Those
two corrections give two distinct valid mixed vectors.

    python3 -B tools/check_three_wrong_readings.py

When some readings are missing and others are wrong

A missing reading costs one check because we know its address. A wrong
reading at an unknown address costs two, one to find the address and one
to find the amount. With six checks, any combination costing six or fewer
is guaranteed recoverable. That includes two missing and two wrong, or
four missing and one wrong.

Fill the missing readings with zero for the calculation. Form the monic
polynomial F(T) with the missing addresses as roots. Apply its coefficients
to successive checks as above. An error at address x now contributes the
same geometric sequence as before, with weight multiplied by F(x). That
kills the missing addresses, leaving the unknown errors and 6 − r checks
when r readings are missing. Decode and remove those errors first, then
use the remaining checks to solve for the values at the known addresses.

The limit is sharp for every number of missing readings from zero to six.
Choose a nonzero valid difference supported on seven positions. To find
one, solve the six check equations on those positions. Every coordinate
of the difference must be nonzero because any six columns are independent.
Erase r of those positions from both valid sets of readings and split the
remaining positions into two nearly equal groups. An observation that
takes one group from each set is consistent with either original, one
unknown error past the guarantee.

    python3 -B tools/check_mixed_damage.py
