Where the mirror holds still

The zeta function has a mirror: the line at one half. Drag the point. Its twin is its reflection in the line, and the function's two values there are reflections of each other as well. A point on the line is its own twin: the line is where the mirror holds still. The Riemann Hypothesis says every zero in the strip sits there.

What am I looking at?

The mirror. The strip is the set of complex numbers with σ between 0 and 1. The mirror sends σ to and leaves the height t alone. The only points it leaves in place are the ones with . So the centre line is the set of fixed points of the mirror, and ½ is the one fixed point of .

The function. Riemann's ξ is zeta with its known factors attached: . In the strip it has exactly the zeros of zeta. It respects the mirror: its value at the twin is the complex conjugate of its value at s, the same number with the sign of its imaginary part flipped. The dial shows the two values as directions, and they are always reflections of each other in the horizontal axis.

What follows for zeros. If ξ is zero at s, it is zero at the twin. So a zero off the line would never come alone: it would have a partner on the other side (and, counting the lower half of the plane, which is the upper half upside down, they would come in fours). The Riemann Hypothesis says there are no such pairs. Every zero is its own twin, a fixed point of the mirror. This is also the first step of the Lean proof announced in November 2026, read from the top: a zero to the left of the line would have a twin to the right, so it is enough to rule out zeros on the right. Is the mirror enough? No. A mirror only promises that zeros off the line come in pairs; it does not forbid them. A mirror is not enough shows a cousin of zeta with the same mirror and a pair of zeros off the line, and How a zero leaves the line shows that pair leaving and coming back.

Why the line is special for counting. A number equal to its own conjugate is real. So along the line ξ is an ordinary real curve, drawn in the third picture after dividing by a positive factor so that it stays in view. Each of the five zeros in view is a place where that curve changes sign, and that is how zeros on the line are found and counted: by watching a sign. Off the line the value is a genuine complex number and no sign is available.
