Why no pair escapes the count

Start at angle 0 with just one Dirichlet L-function. A winding count on the right half of the contour, doubled by reflection, gives 131 zeros. The signs on the center line change in 131 disjoint intervals, putting all these zeros on the line before the dial moves.

We use the two terms A and B of the mixture. A simple zero on the center line stays there under a small change of angle, because its reflected copy must be the same nearby zero. For an off-line pair to appear or disappear inside the band, two zeros must meet on the line. At such a meeting the ratio A/B is stationary. Interval arithmetic finds every stationary point and determines whether a pair leaves or returns.

  • From 0 to 76 there are 18 meetings.
  • From 76 to 177 there are 40.
  • From 177 to 201 there are 8.

The sides at real parts −3 and 4 are zero-free for every angle. The upper edge at 201, and the internal split heights 76 and 177, allow only simple line zeros to cross.
