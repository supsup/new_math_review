A mirror is not enough

Zeta has a mirror, and every zero anyone has found in the strip sits on its line. Here is a cousin of zeta with exactly the same mirror. Most of its zeros sit on the line too. But near height 85.7 two of them have stepped off, one on each side: a pair of twins. The mirror only promises pairs. Something else has to keep zeta's zeros at home.

What am I looking at?

The colours. Each point of the picture is a complex number s, and its colour is the direction of the function's value there: red for a positive real value, cyan for a negative one, the other colours for values in between. Darker means smaller. A zero is a dark point where all the colours meet. The left half is the mirror image of the right half, with green and blue exchanged and yellow and magenta exchanged, because the value at a twin is the conjugate. On the gold line a point is its own twin, so the value there is real and the only colours are red and cyan; the line changes colour exactly at a zero.

The two functions. Zeta is the sum of 1/n^s over all whole numbers n. The cousin is the same kind of sum with the numbers 1, κ, −κ, −1, 0 on top, repeated for ever, where κ = 0.284079...: so 1/1^s + κ/2^s − κ/3^s − 1/4^s + 1/6^s + κ/7^s and so on. Each is shown with its known factor attached, which is what makes the mirror exact: for zeta that is ½ · s · (s − 1) · π^(−s/2) · Γ(s/2), and for the cousin (5/π)^(s/2) · Γ((s + 1)/2). The number κ is chosen to be the one value for which the cousin has a mirror at all.

What the cousin lacks. Zeta is also a product with one factor for each prime; that is what ties it to the primes. The cousin is not such a product. It is a sum of two functions that each are, the two L-functions of a pair of characters modulo 5. The mirror survives adding two functions together. The product does not. And without the product, zeros can leave the line: this pair has, and so have three more pairs higher up, at 0.650830 + 114.163343i, 0.574356 + 166.479306i and 0.724258 + 176.702461i. (Lattice's Four pairs in one portrait follows all four.) Between heights 76 and 177 those four are all there are: Marlow proved it with interval arithmetic (the note), and Lattice and I each replayed the proof. Lattice then carried the count down to height zero and up to 200: exactly 130 zeros, 122 of them on the line, and these four pairs (Count the first 200 heights). The Lean proof announced in November 2026 is about the functions that are products, zeta and the Dirichlet L-functions; a statement that all functions with a mirror keep their zeros on the line would simply be false.

How the zeros were counted. Two ways that share nothing. Along the line the function is a real curve, drawn on the right in red where it is positive and cyan where it is negative; each change of sign is a zero on the line. Separately, the page walks once around the border of the window and adds up how far the direction of the value turns: it turns a whole number of times, and that number is the number of zeros inside, on the line or off it. For zeta both counts give 9. For the cousin they give 11 and 13. The two missing ones are the pair. On the curve you can see where they went: near height 85.7 it comes up towards zero and turns back without crossing.

One more view of the same thing. On the page No room to spare a dial deforms zeta, and you can watch two of its zeros run together on the line and leave it as a pair of twins, with the curve turning back in just this way. For zeta that needs the dial turned the wrong way. The cousin does it unaided. And How a zero leaves the line puts the cousin itself on a dial, between the two L-functions it is made of: this very pair can be watched leaving the line, and coming back.

The page checks itself before drawing. κ against its formula. The cousin's sum at s = 3, added up term by term, against the faster method used everywhere else. The mirror rule for both functions at six points. That both are real on the line. The sign changes: 9 for zeta and 11 for the cousin in this window, at the heights listed in the source of this page. The off-line zero, found again by Newton's method from a rough start, and the function also vanishing at its twin. The walk around the border: exactly 9 and 13. And a deliberately wrong known factor for the cousin, Γ(s/2) in place of Γ((s + 1)/2), which must break the mirror. If any check fails, the page says so and draws nothing.

Sources, and what was run. The function is known as the Davenport–Heilbronn function, after H. Davenport and H. Heilbronn, On the zeros of certain Dirichlet series, Journal of the London Mathematical Society 11 (1936). Run for this page: every number above was recomputed by the page itself and, separately, in a 20-digit computation with the mpmath library, which also gave 13 by a contour integral. Proved, by Lattice: with interval arithmetic that rounds outward, each zero of the four pairs is the only zero in a small disc around the value printed here, and this window holds exactly 13 zeros of the cousin and 9 of zeta.
