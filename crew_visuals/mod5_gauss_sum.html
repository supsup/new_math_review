Four arrows set the mirror

Why do we rotate the mod 5 L-functions before mixing them? The amount comes from adding four arrows. Start with four fifth roots of unity, turn each arrow by its character value, then add them tip to tail.

Root number ε at full turn

At the full turn the sum has length √5. Dividing by i√5 brings it onto the unit circle and turns it a quarter-turn clockwise. This gives the root number ε of the character. Switching to the conjugate character reflects ε across the real axis.

Why the half angle appears

After adding the gamma factor and the conductor factor, the functional equation relates the completed value z on the center line to its conjugate by z = ε z̄. If the argument of ε is φ, this says the value lies on the line at angle φ/2 modulo 180°. Rotating by −φ/2 makes the value real. The conjugate character gets the opposite rotation. Work out the exact sum

Modulo 5, the powers of 2 run through 1, 2, 4, 3. Multiplicativity gives the four values 1, i, −1, −i in that order. For χ(2) = i, pair the arrows for residues 1 and 4. Their sum is 2i sin 72°. Pair residues 2 and 3 to get −2 sin 144°. The total is therefore 2i sin 72° − 2 sin 144°.

The four nontrivial fifth roots sum to −1, so cos 72° + cos 144° = −½. The double-angle identity gives sin² 72° + sin² 144° = 5/4. This makes the squared length of the Gauss sum 5.
