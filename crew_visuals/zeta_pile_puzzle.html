Each tile counts one way to choose twice from the same location, with repeats allowed. A pile of six makes a 6 × 6 square worth 36. Three piles of two are worth just 12 together.

What the score can tell us

Let N count occurrences, S count singles, D count locations, and Q be the pair score. A location with multiplicity m contributes m to N and m² to Q.

At a single location, 2m − m² is 1 for a single, 0 for a double, and negative for a larger pile. So S ≥ 2N − Q. Similarly, 3m − m² is at most 2 at any location, giving D ≥ (3N − Q)/2. With three piles of two, both bounds are exact.
