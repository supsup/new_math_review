Make a polynomial listen to one quartet

A few positive pairs can hide one negative quartet. Choose which pairs to include. Then switch to a polynomial whose roots cancel them all, leaving a negative contribution from the quartet. This small model uses exact arithmetic.

Each tile shows a conjugate pair on the unit circle and its contribution to the test. Click to include or exclude it.

Choose the weights by choosing roots

The weights are the coefficients of a polynomial p(z). A conjugate pair on the unit circle contributes a positive number times |p(z)|². Give the polynomial a root at that pair and its contribution becomes zero. Each pair needs a real quadratic factor. Then one final factor keeps the off-circle quartet’s contribution negative.

With P selected pairs, this construction uses at most 2P + 3 coefficients. That many slots suffice for a negative test in this example. The experiment on the previous page uses the full zeta matrix, where there are infinitely many pair contributions. How the matrix test works.

Follow the exact calculation

The tiles are indexed by k from −3 to 3, with 4 cos θ = k. A pair’s contribution is (4 − k)|p(eiθ)|². The polynomial D(z) multiplies the factors 2z² − kz + 2 over the selected tiles. Each kills its one pair because eiθ + e−iθ = k/2.

For the quartet take w = i/2. Then w + 1/w = −3i/2, and grouping the factors shows D(w)/wP = ∏(−k − 3i) = A + iB. Choose the final factor as 2Az² − 3Bz + 2A. At w this makes the centered value p(w)/wP+1 equal to −3i(A² + B²).

The coefficients read the same forward and backward. Thus p(w)p(1/w) is the square of that centered value. The quartet contributes 4 Re[(2 − w − 1/w)p(w)p(1/w)] = −72(A² + B²)². All factors in the product for A + iB are nonzero, so this is strictly negative. Marlow’s interpolation argument gives a broader construction for a finite list of distinct modes.
