What happens when every sign becomes +1?

A determinant and a permanent pick the same entries of a matrix. The determinant subtracts some picks. The permanent adds them all. Switch to arithmetic mod 2 to see why the two polynomials become identical.

What each sum counts

Pick one allowed arrow from each row so every column gets one arrow. Each vertex now has one arrow entering and one leaving. The arrows close up into disjoint cycles, including single-vertex loops. The permanent counts every such cover. A cover of n vertices with r cycles contributes (−1)n−r to the determinant. One cycle of length k has the sign of k−1 swaps. The Hamiltonian polynomial sums just the covers with one cycle through all vertices.

Start with loops and a swap. The loops contribute +1 and the two-cycle contributes −1. Over the integers this gives determinant 0 and permanent 2. Mod 2, −1 is also +1, so both sums give 0. The argument works for variable entries too, term by term. In characteristic two a polynomial-size determinant circuit therefore also computes the permanent. Lean proof of equality in characteristic two.

When do the two integer values agree?

Every negative cover contributes 2 to the gap between permanent and determinant. If all loops are present, an even-length directed cycle produces a negative cover by using that cycle and leaving loops everywhere else. Conversely, a negative cover has an odd number of even-length cycles, so it contains at least one. Thus on a board with every loop, the two integer values agree exactly when the graph has no even-length directed cycle.

Try a three-vertex ring and add the three loops. The cover with all loops and the cover with the single three-cycle both have positive sign. Add an arrow back along one edge of the ring and the resulting two-cycle allows a negative cover. Watch the extra cycles cancel

Keep vertex A on the permanent side. For every subset S of the other vertices, multiply the determinant of the negated submatrix on S by the permanent of the submatrix on its complement. Use determinant 1 on the empty subset. Add the products to get the Hamiltonian count. Each submatrix keeps the same vertices for its rows and columns. Hamiltonian cycle identity.

The selected cover contributes only when each of its cycles lies wholly on one side. Each cycle put on the determinant side contributes a sign of −1 after negating that submatrix. Moving any one extra cycle pairs a plus and a minus, canceling the cover. A single spanning cycle has only the empty choice, with sign +1.

In characteristic two both factors are determinants. The expression still sums over 2n−1 subsets.

Why the two papers name both polynomials

In Valiant’s model, VP families have polynomial-size arithmetic circuits. VNP allows a sum over every Boolean setting of auxiliary inputs to such a circuit. The recent OpenAI and Anthropic papers both state superpolynomial lower bounds for the directed Hamiltonian cycle polynomial over every field and for the permanent outside characteristic two.

OpenAI. The paper gives an exp(Ω((log n)5/4)) lower bound even for border circuits, where one may take algebraic limits. Its Lean release proves the superpolynomial bounds for exact circuits, including the two named polynomial families. Its circuit model treats constants and inputs as free and allows binary addition, subtraction and multiplication. Paper theorem and projection corollary · Lean scope.

Anthropic. The paper and Lean release use the bound mc(log₂ m)1/3 for both exact and approximating circuits. The Lean model counts variables and constants as gates, uses addition and multiplication, and defines approximation by retaining the constant coefficient of a Laurent series in a parameter, with no negative powers left in the output.
