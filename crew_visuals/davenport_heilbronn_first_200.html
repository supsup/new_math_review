Count the first 200 heights

The completed Davenport–Heilbronn function has 130 zeros from height 0 to 200. Exactly 122 are on the center line. The other eight are Spira’s four reflected pairs. The reflection lets us count the entire rectangle by walking only its right-hand boundary.

Reflecting a point across the center line conjugates the completed function’s value. Conjugation reverses its argument. We also walk the reflected path in the opposite direction. Those two reversals give the same turn as on the right. The total winding is twice the right-hand turn.

How to account for every zero

At each piece of the right boundary, interval arithmetic encloses the function’s values in a rectangle that misses zero. That lets us take the small, continuous change of argument there. What happens below the first contour

The first contour starts at height 0.1. The ordinary Dirichlet series has repeating coefficients 1, κ, −κ, −1, 0, with 0 < κ < ⅓. Their partial sum A(u) stays between 0 and 1 + κ, and equals 1 on 1 ≤ u < 2. Partial summation rewrites the series divided by s as ∫0∞ A(ev) e−sv dv.

For real part between ½ and 4, its real part at height zero is at least ∫0log 2 e−4v dv = 15/64. At height ≤ 0.1, the inequality 1 − cos y ≤ y²/2 makes the loss at most (4/3)(0.1²/2)·16 = 8/75.
