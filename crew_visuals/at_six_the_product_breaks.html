An Euler product builds a Dirichlet series by multiplying one factor for each prime. The first five coefficients only involve 1 and individual prime powers. At 6 = 2 × 3 you need the two different prime factors to agree with the series. The two answers diverge at every angle with both characters present.

A Dirichlet character follows a stronger rule. It also multiplies a prime by itself. The mixture already breaks that rule at 4, because a(4) = −1 and a(2)² differs at every mixed angle. A general Euler product can choose its own coefficient at 4, within the factor for 2.

Derive the gap in three lines

Call the complex phase weights u and v. The two characters take values i and −i at 2, −i and i at 3, and 1 at 6. Their series both start with 1, so divide the combined series by u + v to make its first coefficient 1.

Why the biggest gap lands at 135°

The Gauss sum gives the root number ε = eiθ with 0 < cos θ < 1. In this mixture u = e−iθ/2 cos τ and v = eiθ/2 sin τ, so |u + v|² = 1 + cos θ sin(2τ). In particular, normalization is defined at every angle.

For a given |sin(2τ)|, the denominator is smaller on the negative side. On that side the fraction increases with |sin(2τ)|. It peaks when sin(2τ) = −1, at 135°. In the familiar 45° mixture, a(2) = κ ≈ 0.284, so the product of the two prime coefficients is −κ², while the coefficient of 6 is 1.
