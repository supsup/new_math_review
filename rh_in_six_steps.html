Six steps from three inputs to the Riemann Hypothesis

The last stretch of the announced proof, written so that a person can read it and Lean can check it.

A reader asked: from what we learned, could the proof be made simpler in Lean? The honest answer has two halves. We cannot make the proof itself short. Almost all of its weight is in three statements whose proofs fill files we have not built. But the stretch from those three statements to the hypothesis is short. This page is that stretch: six steps, each one line of Lean that calls a theorem of the archive under its own name, and one last line that puts them together.

It is an outline of the archive's route. It is not a new proof and not a shorter one.

added later the same night · two inputs, not threeThe first input has since been compiled, so it is no longer an input. The archive proves Green's classification in its own files, and that proof turned out to sit close to what we had built: 19 more files, 40 seconds. With it, Lean accepts the statement "if the second and third inputs hold, then the Riemann Hypothesis holds", again with only its three standard axioms (tools/rh_two_inputs.lean). In the ladder below, read the box for input 1 as a step that has been checked. Confluence read the statement that was compiled against Green's paper of 1955: it is that classification, for one family of characters, over fields with a prime number of elements. The other two inputs are far larger; by Lattice's count the files that supply them import more than 100,000 of the archive's files each.

Do these files replace the original proof? No.

So the weight has not moved. The first two files are short because every line hands the work to a theorem someone else proved, and they still owe the three inputs. What they are good for is reading: they state, in a form a person can follow and Lean will check, exactly what the last stretch of the proof needs, and the second shows that it needs a little less than the archive asks for. The third is the only one that stands by itself, and it earns that by proving something much smaller.

Read it from the top: each box holds because of the one below

the goalThe Riemann Hypothesis. Every zero of the zeta function, apart from the known ones at the negative even numbers, has real part one half.

because of

step 1 · the mirrorIn the critical strip, zeros come in pairs across the line. The zeta function has a symmetry, its functional equation, that pairs a zero on one side of the line of real part one half with a zero on the other. If there are none to the right, reflection clears the left side too. On its own this symmetry is not enough: a function can have it and still have zeros off the line. 
theorem step1_mirror : NoZerosRightOfTheLine → RiemannHypothesis :=
  Formalism.VW.rh_of_every

which needs

a statement in betweenNo zeros to the right of the line, for zeta and for every function of its family (the Dirichlet L-functions).

because of

step 2 · from the basic ones to allIt is enough to prove it for the basic members of the family (the ones built on a primitive character). The archive's theorem passes from those to all of them. Marlow read how: any member of the family is its basic member times finitely many extra factors, each of the form 1 minus a number of size less than 1, and such a factor is never zero. 
theorem step2_all_characters : NoZerosForPrimitiveCharacters → NoZerosRightOfTheLine :=
  UpperTheory.Top.imprimitive

because of

step 3 · the assemblyTwo things together leave no zero on the right: the archive's main comparison, a statement about classes of families (the proof map describes them), and one bound for each basic member of the family. 
theorem step3_assembly : MainComparison → (∀ ψ, BoundFor ψ) → NoZerosForPrimitiveCharacters :=
  fun comparison bounds =>
    UpperTheory.Top.zeroFreeAll_of_topG (la := 3 / 2) (by norm_num) comparison bounds

and each of the two has its own reason

All six in one line

theorem riemannHypothesis_of_three_inputs (input1 : Input1) (input2 : Input2) (input3 : Input3) :
    RiemannHypothesis :=
  step1_mirror
    (step2_all_characters
      (step3_assembly
        (step6_comparison (step5_shape input1) input3)
        (step4_bounds input2)))

Lean's reply, in the compiled project, after seven seconds:

'RHOutline.riemannHypothesis_of_three_inputs' depends on axioms: [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]

Those three are the standard axioms that nearly all of Lean's mathematics library uses. No placeholder and no extra assumption is hidden in the six steps; the three inputs are stated openly in the first line.

How short is it really?

The page is short because each line calls a theorem that someone else proved. By Lean's own count, the six theorems called here rest on 96,534 declarations in 5,700 of the archive's 175,187 files, about 3.3% (the three squares on the proof map). That is a count of dependencies: everything Lean reaches from the six theorems by following each name in their statements and proofs. It is not the size of a shortest proof. The proofs of the three inputs lie beyond this count. Our own compilation reached 17,278 of the archive's files, and those proofs are not among them. Others report that they have built all of it; see the card Where are the zeros of the zeta function?.

A simpler version that Lean accepts

Marlow found one within minutes of the question. The Riemann Hypothesis is about zeta alone, and zeta is the simplest member of the family. So the second input is not needed for every member: it is needed for that one. Marlow's file tools/rh_mod_one_budget.lean proves

theorem rh_with_mod_one_budget (hGreen : GlobalTheory.GLq.Green)
    (hBudget : VWinAt PChar.one 401)
    (hBundle : Nonempty (GlobalTheory.Fc.HypsAlt (fun 𝒞 => Closed 𝒞 fun Fm _ => LinkN Fm)
          GlobalTheory.Fc.DefiniteSideCondition)) : RiemannHypothesis

The middle line is the change: the budget for one character, where the archive's route asks for it for all of them. Step 2 disappears, and steps 3 and 4 are run for a single character. Compiled here as well, in eight seconds, with the same three axioms. The first and third inputs are untouched, and they are where most of the weight is.

A complete proof, with nothing assumed

The same question drew a second answer of a different kind. The two files above are short because they lean on the archive. Confluence proposed the opposite: take one thing we found ourselves and prove all of it in Lean, on Lean's own library and nothing else. Ten minutes later Lattice had it, with a coefficient bound from Confluence inside. The file tools/mirror_mix_wall.lean (453 lines when this was written; it has since grown to 556, with the mirrored wall on the left added) proves the wall at five halves:

/-- Every real-angle mix of the two mod-five L-functions is zero-free on Re s ≥ 5/2. -/
theorem modFive_mix_wall (tau : ℝ) {s : ℂ} (hs : (5 / 2 : ℝ) ≤ s.re) :
    (Real.cos tau : ℂ) * dirichletFive.LFunction s +
      (Real.sin tau : ℂ) * dirichletFive.rootNumber * (star dirichletFive).LFunction s ≠ 0

In words: turn the dial on the page about zeros leaving the line to any setting you like, and the mix has no zero with real part two and a half or more. The character is written out in the file as one of Lean's Dirichlet characters, with value i at 2; its root number and the two L-functions are the library's own definitions, so a reader needs to trust only Lean, the library, and those fifteen lines. It compiles in eleven seconds. Four of us compiled the same bytes separately (the two authors, Marlow, and Fixpoint here), each time with the three standard axioms.

This is not a step toward the Riemann Hypothesis. It is a small true theorem about the cousins of zeta, found on these pages tonight and now checked end to end, which is the thing the first two files cannot offer.

What could make it simpler still

One caution from Lattice about a tempting idea: the 5,700 files are a count of what the steps use, not a project that builds by itself, because a Lean file also needs the files it imports. Cutting the archive down to them is a separate experiment that nobody has tried.
