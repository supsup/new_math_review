How the repair works

At position j, take the input at j once and the inputs at j minus 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 9 and 14 twice. Positions wrap around at 16 and every sum is reduced modulo 17.

Write those taps as a polynomial H. Its only roots among the powers of 3 modulo 17 are 3¹ through 3⁷. At those seven addresses any valid mixed vector evaluates to zero. Any seven columns of these power checks are independent by interpolation. Two different valid mixes therefore differ in at least eight positions.

A wrong value can hide a discrepancy in either of two candidate mixes. A missing one hides a discrepancy at one location. That is why each wrong reading spends two units and each missing reading spends one. The recovery guarantee holds when their total is at most seven.

Once the mixed readings are repaired, the possible input differences have the form 3−j·P(3−j) for a polynomial P of degree at most six. Your seven probes determine it. Ordinary invertibility also has a quick check. The board reduces to the identity modulo 2, since all the neighbor coefficients are even.

At eight units, change a single unprobed input. Exactly eight mixed readings change. A report using four readings from each case in those eight positions fits both inputs with four wrong readings.

Why sixteen is the minimum with weights 1 and 2

This claim is for an eight-tap 0–1–2 circulant invertible over the rationals, with a seven-dimensional kernel modulo a prime p and every choice of seven probes successful. Eight places require a full row support. Modular rank one then makes each shift a scalar multiple of the preceding row. The two available values force period at most two over an odd prime. Modulo 2, rank one forces every row to be the same. In either case the integer row pattern is periodic and the ordinary matrix is singular.

For n ≥ 9 the seven-probe requirement forces p ≥ max(8, n−6) from the two-row minors of a systematic kernel basis. With u doubled taps, each row has sum s = 8+u and squared norm r = 8+3u. Its determinant is divisible by both s and p⁷. The first singular value is s, and the other squared singular values sum to nr−s². The arithmetic–geometric mean bound leaves a finite set of primes.

p2(7−v)(n−1)n−1 ≤ (nr−s²)n−1, with v = 1 if p divides s and 0 otherwise.

Through size 13 no prime passes those bounds. At size 14, every candidate with modular corank seven is singular over the rationals. At size 15, each candidate surviving both rank tests has a singular seven-probe minor. The replay checks every remaining coefficient pattern and all 11,440 sets of seven probes on the winning board.
