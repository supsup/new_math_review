Which odd primes still need a bound?
====================================

The modulus-one route to the Riemann hypothesis asks for a budget
on certain Galois cohomology groups. Think of each coefficient
prime ℓ and each twist index j as a line in that budget. The
charge on a line is a module length multiplied by log ℓ. At odd
primes there are two charges, called Plus and Minus. Their names
refer to eigenspaces for complex conjugation, with the signs
depending on the parity of j.

The previous calculation covers the charge at 2. The archive's
individual Stickelberger estimate covers all the small odd Minus
charges, even at primes ramified in the host. For the large primes
there is an exact way to describe each finite torsion layer using
an ideal class group. The class-group description identifies the
quantity that would need a uniform bound.

Paying for small odd Minus
--------------------------

Let K be a CM host of degree D embedded in the cyclotomic field
of conductor N. The archive's StickBound gives finite Minus length
at every odd ℓ and every j ≥ 1. It bounds the weighted length by

    C ℓ D² (j+1) log²(jℓN).

The archive's Procyc condition holds at every odd ℓ by its first
clause. There is no conductor coprimality condition in this step.

For any cutoff L ≥ 2, a finite set of odd primes ≤ L contains at
most L primes. For 1 ≤ j < t each j+1 is at most t. Bounding each
factor and summing gives

    small odd Minus charge ≤ C D² t² L² log²(t L N).

The Lean theorem in tools/zeta_odd_small_cutoff.lean checks this
finite-sum inequality and the individual finiteness claim using
the archive's own definition of Minus length. Its second
theorem assembles the full budget from this estimate and three
separate bounds at 2, for Plus, and for large odd Minus.

For the selected modulus-one windows, let x be the scale and take

    L = floor(exp(x^ε/8)).

The charge allowed in the original budget is exp(x^ε/2). Here
L² ≤ exp(x^ε/4). Also D ≤ 8x^(2+8ε), t ≤ x^400 and
log N = O(x^(ε/4)) by the host and prime-pair estimates. Thus
D²t² log²(t L N) grows at most polynomially in x. At fixed ε,
the ratio of the small odd Minus charge to the allowed budget
tends to zero. The dyadic charge O_ε(x^805) has the same property.

Eventually L exceeds t+1 and every prime divisor of N. In
particular q and d, the two odd ramified primes of K, are already
covered on the Minus side. A sufficient budget on each selected
window is then finiteness of the Plus lengths and, for every
finite set of odd primes, a bound of half the original allowance
on all its Plus charges and its Minus charges at ℓ > L. The
absorbed charges fit in the other half after discarding an
initial range of x. The selected windows remain cofinal.

A class-group description at large primes
-----------------------------------------

For an odd ℓ unramified in K, take j with ℓ−1 not dividing j.
Let A be ℚ_ℓ/ℤ_ℓ twisted by the inverse cyclotomic character to
power j. For a ≥ 1 put B = A[ℓ^a] and E = K(μ_(ℓ^a)).
The strict kernel T(K,A) consists of the global H¹ classes
that vanish on decomposition groups over ℓ and on inertia
at every other finite place. It is the archive's Sha group.

Write Δ = Gal(E/K) and Cl(E) for the ordinary ideal class group.
There is an isomorphism

    T(K,A)[ℓ^a] ≃ Hom_Δ(Cl(E), ℤ/ℓ^a(−j)).

Here Hom_Δ means that applying a cyclotomic automorphism to an
ideal class acts on its image by the inverse j-th power of that
automorphism's cyclotomic value. The formula holds for every
finite layer a and for the full kernel, so either sign component
can be taken afterward. Every remaining prime ℓ > L satisfies
the two hypotheses for every twist in the budget.

The proof has three steps.

1. Over each local field K_v at ℓ, the cyclotomic extension is
   totally ramified with the full group (ℤ/ℓ^a)ˣ. This follows
   because K_v over ℚ_ℓ is unramified and ℚ_ℓ(μ_(ℓ^a)) over ℚ_ℓ
   is totally ramified. In particular Δ is the full group,
   each v has one prime above it in E, and a tame cyclotomic
   element acts on A by a scalar whose difference from 1 is
   a unit. Thus A has no fixed elements globally or locally at
   ℓ. The cohomology sequence for multiplication by ℓ^a on A
   identifies the global ℓ^a-torsion in H¹ with H¹ for B.
   It also gives injection on H¹ for the local groups used
   here. Over ℓ this uses the absence of fixed elements.
   Away from ℓ, inertia acts trivially, so its fixed module
   A is divisible and multiplication by ℓ^a is surjective
   there. Consequently T(K,A)[ℓ^a] equals the strict kernel
   with coefficients B.

2. The order-(ℓ−1) subgroup of Δ has zero invariants in B,
   by that same tame element. Its higher cohomology with
   coefficients B is zero by averaging. The Hochschild–Serre
   sequence then gives H¹(Δ,B) = H²(Δ,B) = 0, including at
   wild layers a > 1. Inflation and restriction identify the
   global H¹ for B with Δ-equivariant homomorphisms on the
   absolute Galois group of E, where B has trivial action.
   The local restriction at ℓ is injective by the same
   inflation and restriction argument, since the local
   quotient is all of Δ. Away from ℓ, E/K is unramified and
   the inertia groups agree.

3. Class field theory now identifies the everywhere-unramified
   homomorphisms upstairs with maps from Cl(E). The extra split
   condition at ℓ is automatic on this component. Each prime
   over ℓ is fixed by Δ, so its ideal class is fixed. An
   equivariant map sends that class into the zero fixed part
   of B. By reciprocity it therefore kills its Frobenius.
   The field E is totally imaginary, so the ordinary ideal
   class group is the one used here.

The archive's signs can be read from Cl(E) as well. Since K and
ℚ(μ_(ℓ^a)) are disjoint over ℚ, complex conjugation of K lifts
to the automorphism of E that fixes μ_(ℓ^a). Using that lift,
it acts on the maps through the ideal classes and acts
trivially on the target B. Take its eigenvalue (−1)^j for Plus
and (−1)^(j+1) for Minus.

Real and relative class groups
------------------------------

Complex conjugation of the whole field E is the product of
the lift fixing the roots of unity and the element −1 of Δ.
On a Δ-equivariant map, the latter acts on the image by
(−1)^j. The archive's Plus maps therefore come from the
eigenvalue +1 of full complex conjugation on the class group
of E, and Minus maps from eigenvalue −1.

On the ℓ-primary class group, these eigenspaces are the class
group of the real subfield and the relative class group,
respectively. To see the first identification, compose the
norm and extension of ideals in the two orders. They give
multiplication by 2 on the real class group and 1+c on the
class group upstairs, where c is full complex conjugation.
Since 2 is invertible on ℓ-primary groups, norm identifies
the +1 group upstairs with the real ℓ-primary class group.
The −1 group is the kernel of that norm.

For ℓ > L at late windows, the j in the budget have distinct
residues modulo ℓ−1. At each finite layer they therefore use
distinct eigenspaces for the order-(ℓ−1) tame subgroup in those
same class groups. In particular one can test vanishing of a
line at a = 1. The strict cohomology group is ℓ-primary torsion,
because a continuous cocycle has finite image in the discrete
coefficient module, and any nonzero ℓ-primary torsion group
has nonzero ℓ-torsion. Bounding a nonzero length still requires
control beyond the first layer.

One nonzero line in the absorbed part
-------------------------------------

At ℓ = 37 and j = 31, the numerator of B_32 =
−7709321041217/510 is divisible by 37. Herbrand–Ribet gives a
nonzero map on the class group of ℚ(μ_37) to the character
ω^(1−32) = ω^(−31). The proof above also applies over ℚ in
this example, since E is already totally imaginary. It gives
a nonzero strict class over ℚ. Restriction to a modulus-one
host is injective on this class because corestriction times
restriction acts by the host degree, a power of two. The
local zero conditions are preserved by restriction.

This restricted class is invariant under the field Galois
group. For j = 31 the invariant sign is Minus. The example
shows that a line can have a genuine charge and still be
covered by the small-prime estimate.

One nonzero Plus line from a cubic
----------------------------------

For a Plus example, take ℓ = 3, j = 1 and K₀ = ℚ(√−419).
A small cubic produces a nontrivial class without computing a
class number. The polynomial X³ − 2X² − 7X − 1 has no rational
root, and its discriminant is 1257 = 3·419. Its splitting
field has group S₃ and quadratic subfield F = ℚ(√1257).

At both 3 and 419 the polynomial has a simple root and a
distinct double root modulo the prime. Hensel's lemma lifts
the simple root, so the local splitting field is at most
quadratic. Its local Galois group therefore meets A₃
trivially. At every other prime the discriminant is a unit.
The discriminant is positive, so the three roots are real.
The splitting field is thus everywhere unramified over F,
with cyclic group of order 3.

By class field theory this gives a quotient Cl(F) → ℤ/3.
The nonidentity automorphism of F acts by inversion on that
quotient, because a transposition in S₃ inverts A₃.
Now F is the real subfield of E = K₀(μ₃). Pulling the quotient
back by norm gives a nonzero map from Cl(E), since composing
extension and norm acts by 2 on Cl(F). On F the cyclotomic
involution and the lift of conjugation of K₀ both act by its
nonidentity automorphism. The map therefore has the required
cyclotomic twist and the archive's Plus sign (−1)^j.

The class persists by restriction in any power-of-two host
containing K₀. The nontrivial sign under conjugation is
preserved because restriction commutes with field
automorphisms. This gives a concrete small odd Plus term
of the kind the remaining estimate must allow.

Evidence and sources
--------------------

The finite-sum theorem uses the archive at 6193bb5, Lean 4.33.1
and Mathlib 0df444a. With the archive imports on LEAN_PATH, run

    lean --tstack=400000 -DautoImplicit=false \
      tools/zeta_odd_small_cutoff.lean

Lean reports propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound for both
theorems. The cutoff at the host scale and the finite-layer class-group
description above are paper arguments.

In the archive, see OddResidueBounds/ZeroMemUnrHom.lean for
StickBound, OddResidueBounds/ThmBcOfPsiBound.lean for its producer,
and LengthEqAddEigen.lean for the signs. The general archive
route bounds the entire Minus sum using PsiBound through
thmBcFree_of. PrimitiveFrobeniusFixed/Iv.lean produces PsiBound
from Poitou–Tate duality and unramified orthogonality. The
general Plus producer is pointHV_of_counts in
HeightAndVanishing/JointWinOfCounts.lean. This cutoff uses
StickBound alone for the covered odd charges.

See zeta_two_input_cuts.txt for the cofinal window hypothesis,
zeta_dyadic_class_numbers.txt for the estimate at 2, and
zeta_budget_odd_parts.txt for the sign decomposition.

For class-group and cyclotomic-layer context, see Validire,
Capitulation for even K-groups in the cyclotomic Zp-extension,

For the irregular example, see Ribet, A modular construction of
