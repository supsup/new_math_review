How the Farey determinant counts factor chains

The same 0–1 matrix gives two very different answers. Its permanent counts every ordered factorization. Its determinant subtracts the factorizations with an odd number of factors. At order 12 the count is 28 and the signed sum is −2.

Why all the cycles go through 1

In a Redheffer matrix, row i has a 1 in column j whenever i divides j. Every row also has a 1 in column 1. An arrow along a strict divisibility step always increases the number. To close a nontrivial cycle, the final arrow must go back to 1. So a cycle cover consists of at most one cycle with several vertices, and loops on everything else. Factor 12 as 2 × 2 × 3 and the cycle is 1 → 2 → 4 → 12 → 1. It has four vertices and takes three swaps, so it contributes −1 to the determinant. The factors 3 × 4 and 4 × 3 give different cycles. The factorization of 1 uses no factors and gives the cover of all loops.

The sum over factorizations of a fixed q, alternating by length, is its Möbius number. For q = 12 the three groups contribute −1 + 4 − 3 = 0. The first Farey harmonic sums these same Möbius numbers. Farey circle and determinant.

Change the signs, change the zeta function expression

Give a factorization with r factors the weight tr. For fixed r, the Dirichlet series of its counts is (ζ(s) − 1)r. Each factor can be any integer at least two. Sum the geometric series with the weight tr to get 1 / (1 − t(ζ(s) − 1)), for Re s sufficiently large. At t = −1 this is 1/ζ(s). At t = +1 it is 1/(2 − ζ(s)). Ordered factorizations, Table 1.

Follow the same chains for any fixed Farey harmonic

Use the weighted Farey board from the circle page. A chain ending at q accumulates χ(q) from its divisibility edges. Its return to 1 multiplies that by the first-column entry gq = ∑d | k, dq ≤ N dχ(d). To weight the chain by tr in a determinant, multiply each strict divisibility entry by −t. The r determinant swaps cancel the r new minus signs.

For the successive increments as N grows, the fixed harmonic multiplies the Dirichlet series by ∑d | k χ(d)d1−s: a chain ending at q gains the d summand at N = dq. The denominator becomes 1 − t(L(s,χ) − 1), since the character weights in each chain multiply. The replay checks ordinary and weighted determinants at five values of t. Exact replay.

Why the alternating choice is special

For ordinary ζ and any fixed harmonic, every weight t except 0 and −1 rules out a power-saving bound O(Nβ) with β < 1. Marlow noticed the obstruction. The denominator vanishes wherever ζ(s) takes the nonzero value 1 + 1/t. Voronin’s theorem gives such points in every vertical substrip of 1/2 < Re s < 1. The harmonic numerator’s prime-power factors can vanish only on Re s = 1, so each point in the open strip gives a pole.

To obtain the point, approximate a small affine function that takes 1 + 1/t at the center of a disc inside the chosen substrip. The affine function stays away from zero on Voronin’s larger disc. Rouché’s theorem then gives the same number of crossings of 1 + 1/t inside the small disc. If the sums had the proposed bound, partial summation would make their Dirichlet series holomorphic on Re s > β, contradicting such a pole. The case t = 0 leaves just the bounded contribution of the empty factorization for fixed k. The case t = −1 recovers the fixed-harmonic RH criterion.
