A clock and a shift

Two moves on a ring of dots almost commute: do them in the other order and every hand is off by the same fixed turn. That one rule leaves no freedom at all. Anything moved by a shift and a clock that each come back to the start after p steps, and that obey the rule with the same fixed turn, is made of copies of this ring. Over the real numbers that is the theorem of Stone and von Neumann, the reason quantum mechanics has only one way to pair position with momentum. The archive of the announced proof of the Riemann Hypothesis proves it over the p-adic numbers, and Lean has checked that proof on our own machine. This page is the small finite model, where you can see why.

Dots:

What you are looking at

  1. A ring of p dots, some lit, each lit dot with a hand. A hand points at one of p positions round its dot, like the hand of a clock with p hours. The size of a dot is its brightness.
  2. Two moves. The shift carries every dot, hand and all, one place round the ring. The clock leaves the dots where they are and turns their hands: the hand of dot number j goes forward j steps (times the fixed turn you chose). Dot 0 is not turned, dot 1 a little, dot 2 twice as much.
  3. They almost commute. Clock then shift, or shift then clock: the same dots end up lit, with the same brightness. But in the second order every hand is a fixed number of steps ahead. A dot that is shifted first is clocked in its new place, one place further on, so it is turned one unit more.
  4. That rule is the whole group. Any string of shifts and clocks, however long, comes to "shift a places, clock b times, and turn every hand c steps". There are p choices of each, so moves in all. Try it below.
  5. And the rule allows only one picture. Suppose something else is moved about by a "shift" and a "clock" that each come back to the start after p steps and obey the same rule with the same fixed turn: another space of pictures, built any way you like, that cannot be cut into smaller pieces moved separately. Then it is this ring over again, described in other coordinates. The count further down shows why there is no room for anything else.

Try any string of moves

Why there is only one

A general fact about a finite group of moves: list its basic ways of acting, the ones that cannot be cut into smaller pieces, and square the size of each. The squares add up to the number of moves in the group, exactly. So the list can be checked for completeness by counting.

The page also runs a second test that the ring cannot be cut into smaller pieces, and counts the classes of moves, which a second general fact says must equal the number of basic ways of acting. Both agree with the list.

What Lean checked, and what this page is not

The archive's statement is GlobalTheory.Published.StoneVonNeumann, described on card 13: over a p-adic field (more exactly, a non-archimedean local field in which 2 is not zero), take a finite-dimensional space with a non-degenerate alternating form and a non-trivial additive character ψ. The Heisenberg group built from them has an irreducible smooth representation in which the centre acts through ψ, and any two such are isomorphic.

This page is the same statement with the whole numbers mod p in place of the p-adic numbers: the space is the pairs (shift count, clock count); the form is , which says how much the two orders differ; and ψ is the plain turn, the move that turns every hand t steps. (Write a move as shift a, clock b, turn c. Replacing c by turns the page's rule for combining moves into the formula on the card. The page checks this for 3, 5 and 7 dots. The division by 2 is why 2 must not be zero.) It is a model for seeing the idea. It is not the theorem Lean checked, and the count above is a proof only of the finite statement. The numbers of dots offered are odd primes for that reason.

Sources, and what was run

Everything on this page is computed by the page, with whole numbers mod p. A separate program, tools/check_clock_and_shift.py, builds the real p by p matrices of complex numbers, multiplies them as matrices, and finds the group they generate and its classes by brute force; the page agrees with it for every p and fixed turn it covers.
