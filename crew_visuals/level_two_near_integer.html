Almost a whole number

A near-integer that survives squaring

The chart of near-integers has tall bars at 58 and 232. They are related. Squaring E = eπ√58 multiplies its small error, but almost exactly by enough to shift the answer by 8,744. An integer coefficient in a modular series explains the shift.

How the correction appears

For 58, use a modular function with the expansion H(q) = 1/q + 104 + 4,372q + 96,256q² + …. At q = 1/E its value is exactly 24,591,257,856. Subtracting 104 gives the first integer M.

M = E + 4,372/E + 96,256/E² + …

When you square this, the product of E and 4,372/E appears twice. That is exactly 8,744. Subtract it and the remaining error starts at 192,512/E, about 0.00000782847. Since E² = eπ√232, this explains the 232 bar.

Why H is an integer in these examples

For each n = 22, 37, 58 the imaginary quadratic field of discriminant −4n has two reduced forms. One is (1, 0, n). The other has leading coefficient 2, so its lattice has parameter τ = (−b + i√(4n))/4, with b = 0 or 2. The parameter 2τ differs from the first lattice parameter by an integer. Their j values are therefore the two roots of the class polynomial. Its sum and product are integers by complex multiplication. The class polynomial and the archive’s Lean theorem

Put t = (η(τ)/η(2τ))24, using the Dedekind eta function. The two degree-2 isogeny formulas are j(τ) = (t + 256)³/t² and j(2τ) = (t + 16)³/t. With H = t + 128 + 4096/t, their sum is H² − 207H + 3456 and their product is (H + 144)³. Maier uses the coordinate x₂ = 4096/t. Maier’s level-two formulas, Appendix

The sum relation gives H degree at most two over the rationals. The product relation makes H + 144 a cube root of an integer. Here q is real and t is real by the product t = q−1∏j ≥ 1(1 + qj)−24. A real cube root of an integer has degree one or three. It follows that H is an integer.

Extend the calculation to cubes and higher powers

Write F = H − 104. The series for F² − 8,744 starts with q−2, followed only by positive powers. The series for F³ − 13,116F − 288,768 starts with q−3, again followed only by positive powers. For 37, q is negative. Multiply the first and third power formulas by −1 to approximate the positive exponential.

For any positive integer k, start with Fk and remove every other nonpositive term using the previously corrected powers. The coefficients are integers, so each corrected value is an integer. The approximation improves as |q| goes to zero for a fixed k. At a fixed lattice the size of the remainder depends on k, as the cube for 22 illustrates.

Ramanujan recorded the first-power approximations for 22, 37 and 58. This card uses the two class-polynomial roots and the level-two series to explain the integer corrections.
