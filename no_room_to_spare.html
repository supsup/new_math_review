No room to spare

Zeta sits on a dial. Every setting has its own wave, and each place the wave crosses is a zero on the line. Turn the dial down and two crossings slide together, touch, and lift off: that pair of zeros has left the line. The Riemann Hypothesis says that at zeta's own setting, 0, no zero has left. A theorem of 2018 says that at every setting below 0, some pair already has. So 0 would be exactly the edge.

What am I looking at?

The dial. The zeta function belongs to a family of functions , one for each number t; the family goes back to de Bruijn and Newman, whose names the constant below carries. Setting is zeta itself, in the standard form that keeps only the zeros that matter (heights 14.13, 21.02, 25.01, …). Turning the dial up never takes a zero off the line: once every zero is on it, they all stay. Turning it down lets zeros collide.

The three panels share one horizontal axis, the height of a zero. Top: the zeros at the current setting, as dots on the line or off it. Middle: the wave at that setting (scaled so every crossing shows). Bottom: every setting at once. Each gold curve is one zero's path; where two paths meet, the pair leaves the line and continues, dashed red, off it. Drag up and down in the bottom panel to turn the dial.

The constant. Newman showed there is one number Λ so that every zero is on the line exactly when . The Riemann Hypothesis is the statement . Newman guessed ; Rodgers and Tao proved it (arXiv:1801.05914, 2018). Before November 2026 the known range was : Polymath proved 0.22 (arXiv:1904.12438), later improved to 0.2 by Platt and Trudgian (arXiv:2004.09765). If the announced proof of the Riemann Hypothesis stands, Λ is exactly 0: true, with no room to spare.

Why does this picture stay calm until t = …? Because it shows only the first five zeros, and they are far apart. The reason usually given: higher up, zeros crowd together, and a close pair leaves much sooner. A rough rule, which this page tests on its own two collisions: two neighbours a distance g apart on this picture's own scale (twice the height) meet near . The famous near miss at height 7005 (two zeros 0.0377 apart in height) would by that rule go near , some five thousand times sooner than the first pair here. Lattice computed the actual meeting separately, at high precision: at height 7005.08 (tools/check_lehmer_heat.py; a plain check without libraries, tools/check_near_miss_meeting.py, agrees within 0.000000003 in t). For a pair this close the rule is almost exact. Lattice's close view of that pair as it meets: Where two zeta zeros meet. What the 2018 theorem proves is the limit: no setting below 0, however close to it, is safe.
