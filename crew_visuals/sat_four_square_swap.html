How to read the second board and find all four shapes

Each cell on the second board compares two rows of the solution board. For the overlap score, square each count and add the squares. The diagonal counts each row’s overlap with itself.

Rank counts independent rows. Modulo 2, a pair of ones adds to zero. The shared-completion board is the real Gram matrix AA⊤ of the solution table A. Its trace is 9 and its squared Frobenius norm is the overlap score Q. Cauchy–Schwarz on the nonzero eigenvalues gives ordinary rank at least 81/Q. The two buttons with score 39 show why that estimate can leave room for different ranks.

There are 384 ways to choose the four columns from their totals. Exactly 27 also have the required row totals. Group them by permuting the three rows of total 2 and the two columns of total 3, and you get the four arrangements you can try here. Any 0–1 board can be read as a truth table, with a 1 marking a satisfying assignment.
