Why 24 becomes 16 or 20

Start in row 1 with A, B and D. Shift that pattern one point for each new row, wrapping around after G. Among the chosen positions 0, 1, 3, their ordered differences modulo 7 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 exactly once. This means any two different rows meet at one point. The line-and-point pattern is the Fano plane.

If the board is A, the matrix of row overlaps is AAT = 2I + J, with J the all-ones matrix. Its eigenvalues are 9 once and 2 six times. Since its determinant is det(A)², the board has absolute determinant √(9 × 2⁶) = 24. Swapping all zeros and ones gives four shared points with itself and two with each other row. The new overlap matrix is 2I + 2J, yielding √(16 × 2⁶) = 32.

A one-square edit multiplies the determinant by 1 + δ(A⁻¹)ji, where δ is +1 to turn a zero on or −1 to turn a one off. From the overlap matrix, A⁻¹ = ½AT − ⅙J, giving 20 for an addition and 16 for a deletion. For the four-point complement, the same calculation gives ½AT − ¼J with A now the complement. Either edit brings the determinant to 24.
