Why the blanks vanish

In the 60-report, five-wire mixer, report i has address zi = 35−i and multiplier 42−i, all modulo 61. Check Sj weights each reading by its multiplier times zij, for j = 0, 1, 2, 3. All four checks are zero for the sent readings. The received readings differ only at the blanks and the wrong reports.

Call the two blank addresses x and y. In a new sum, multiply the weight at address z by (z − x)(z − y). The two blanks then have weight zero. Expanding the product gives the two combinations below.

T0 = S2 − (x + y)S1 + xyS0
T1 = S3 − (x + y)S2 + xyS1

With one wrong report, T0 is its weighted error and T1 is the same amount times its address. Divide to get the address, then undo the weights to get the correction. With the wrong report fixed, the first two original sums give two equations for the two missing readings.

Why a wrong reading uses two checks

Suppose the received readings have s blanks and at most e wrong visible values. Two plausible originals could differ at all s blanks and at the e corrections proposed by each original. That is at most s + 2e positions in all. The four checks force these disagreements to be zero if there are at most four of them. The first d checks on d distinct addresses form an invertible Vandermonde matrix with scaled columns. So one known blank costs one check and one unknown wrong value costs two.

Five positions can support a nonzero difference that passes all four checks. In the two-error preset the true explanation and the decoder’s one-error explanation agree on every received position except their three distinct proposed corrections. With the two blanks there are five places for the recovered readings to differ. Both explanations pass all four checks.
