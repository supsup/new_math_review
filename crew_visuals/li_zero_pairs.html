What each zero adds to Li’s sum

Send a zeta zero ρ to w = (ρ−1)/ρ. The critical line becomes a circle. Powers of a point on that circle can change its angle, and every conjugate pair gives a nonnegative contribution to Li’s coefficients.

Li’s criterion says RH holds exactly when all the coefficients λn are nonnegative. Each coefficient adds the contributions from the full set of zeros. The off-line quartet here uses invented points with the same symmetries as zeta zeros. Li’s 1997 paper.

Why the critical line gives a circle

The magnitude of w is the distance from ρ to 1 divided by the distance from ρ to 0. Those distances agree on the vertical line halfway between 0 and 1. A conjugate pair gives 2(1 − Re wn), which is nonnegative for a point on the unit circle. At n = 1, summing 1/ρ and its conjugate gives 1/(t² + ¼).

The closed form for λ₁ comes from the logarithmic derivative of the completed zeta function ξ at 1. The derivative of s(s−1)ζ(s) contributes 1+γ there. The factor π−s/2Γ(s/2) contributes −½ log π − γ/2 − log 2.

Where the negative contribution comes from

Reflection ρ ↦ 1−ρ maps w to 1/w. For the synthetic ρ = 0.8 + 0.4i, the images are i/2 and −2i. Add their conjugates. At n = 4 the two powers are 1/16 and 16, so the quartet contributes 4−2(1/16+16) = −225/8.
