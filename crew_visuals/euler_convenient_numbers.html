Euler's sixty-five convenient numbers

In 1778 Euler found a way to prove that a large number is prime by writing it as x² + n·y². It works only for certain n, which he called numeri idonei, convenient numbers. He found 65 of them, the largest being 1848, and none beyond. Whether there are more has been open ever since. In 1973 Weinberger showed that there are at most two more, and none at all if certain L-functions have no zero just to the left of 1. The announced proof of the Riemann Hypothesis ends with a statement that contains this. So if that proof stands, Euler's list is complete.

1 · Euler's test

Take a convenient n and an odd m with no factor in common with n. List the ways to write m = x² + n·y² with x, y ≥ 0. If there is exactly one, and in it x and y have no common factor, then m is prime. For other n the same test can be fooled.

2 · Why some n are convenient

Gauss explained Euler's numbers. The expressions a·x² + 2b·xy + c·y² with ac − b² = n fall into classes (two are in the same class when a change of variables turns one into the other), and the classes fall into genera, according to the signs of a few simple characters on the numbers they take. Each class has one reduced member, with 2|b| ≤ a ≤ c. A number n is convenient exactly when every genus holds a single class. Then a prime is pinned to x² + n·y² by its genus alone, which is what makes Euler's test sound.

4 · Is the list complete?

  1. Euler, 1778, by hand: exactly 65 convenient numbers below 10,000, the largest 1848.
  2. Chowla, 1934: only finitely many in all.
  3. Weinberger, 1973 (his Theorem 1, p. 119): at most one imaginary quadratic field with discriminant below −5460 has a class group of exponent exactly 2, that is, one in which every element squares to the identity but not every element is the identity. (Exponent 1, class number one, was already settled: those are the nine fields of Heegner, Baker and Stark.) Exponent at most 2 is the same as one class per genus. He rules that one field out too, provided that no L(s, χ₋d) has a zero on the short real interval 1 − 1/(4 log d) ≤ s < 1, for every d from about 2·10¹¹ on; machine computations by Lehmer and Swift cover the smaller d. Kani turns this into a count (Corollary 23): at most 67 convenient numbers, and exactly 65 under the hypothesis. The often repeated "at most one more" is an old slip; it is at most two (Kani, Remark 24(a)).
  4. Which hypothesis. Weinberger needs only that the quadratic L-functions have no real zero just to the left of 1. That is weaker than the generalized Riemann hypothesis, which is how Kani's restatement puts it. The announced proof's last file states the full hypothesis for every Dirichlet L-function (the statement DirichletLNonvanishingRightOfHalf, the archive's theorem LFunction_ne_zero_of_half_lt_re), which contains Weinberger's. So the list is complete if that proof stands. We have not checked that part of the archive: what we compiled is the last stretch, from two stated inputs.
  5. Our search: no convenient number between 1849 and 5,000,000, by two independent methods (below). It matters for a second route. Under the full hypothesis, a bound of Louboutin (quoted by Kani, Remark 24(b)) gives more than one class per genus for every fundamental discriminant of size at least 2·10⁷. Why 5,000,000 is enough: by Kani's Corollary 14, a convenient n above 288 is either squarefree with n ≡ 1 or 2 mod 4, or four times such a squarefree convenient number m with m ≡ 2 mod 4. Either way the squarefree number m (n itself, or n/4) has fundamental discriminant exactly −4m, and has one class per genus. So Louboutin's bound forces 4m < 2·10⁷, that is m < 5,000,000, where the search finds only Euler's numbers; and n = 4m is then at most 4·1848, inside Euler's own range. So that route needs no other machine computation. Weinberger's own route needs only the weaker hypothesis, but also Lehmer's and Swift's computations up to about 2·10¹¹, which we have not redone.

The two methods. Grube's criterion (Kani, Proposition 17(a)): n is convenient exactly when no reduced form a·x² + 2b·xy + c·y² with ac − b² = n has 0 < 2b < a < c, since such a form and its mirror image are two different classes, each the other's inverse. Genus count: count the classes, h(−4n), directly, and compare with the number of genera, 2^(μ−1), where μ depends only on the odd primes dividing n and on n mod 8 (Cox, Proposition 3.11). The two share no code. The replay tools/check_euler_convenient.py runs both, and tools/idoneal_search.c runs the first to 5,000,000.
