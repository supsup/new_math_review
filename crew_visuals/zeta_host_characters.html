When a character changes the host field

The archive uses a different host field for a general Dirichlet character. Some characters leave the field the same. Others enlarge it, but that can mean either more primes above p or a bigger residue field. Try the cubic character with 3 and 13.

Two different orders matter

The character adds a cyclic quotient of the cyclotomic Galois group. Its size on the part that still fixes the old host field gives the degree of the enlargement. Its order on the one element corresponding to p gives the extra residue-field condition at that prime.

For the cubic character modulo 7, the field is three times as large. At 3, the character value has order three, so that factor of three goes into the residue degree. At 13, its value is one, so the factor of three goes into the number of primes instead. In both examples the real tower has the same local degree at each step.

The even quadratic character modulo 8 gives a different example. Its quadratic field is already in the real tower, so there is no enlargement. The odd quadratic character modulo 4 adds i and doubles the field.

Where the enlargement formula comes from

Write the conductor of ψ as 2am, where m is odd. By the Chinese remainder theorem, ψ splits into ψ₂ and ψm. Assume that 2a divides the tower modulus and that m is coprime to the two odd conductor primes. Automorphisms fixing the modulus-one host field have dyadic coordinate ±1 and arbitrary coordinate modulo m, independently of the two quadratic tests. Adding ψ = 1 therefore cuts that group by a factor h = lcm(ord ψ₂(−1), |im ψm|). This is the enlargement in the chart. For a fixed character, those divisibility and coprimality conditions hold in sufficiently large windows. Archive’s IsChiField.

Why the count settles down

At an unramified prime, Frobenius acts by raising roots of unity to the pth power. On the host field it returns to the identity once all four conditions are met. Thus the residue degree is the least common multiple of the real-tower degree, the order of ψ(p), and the two quadratic sign orders. The last two are at most two. Cyclotomic Frobenius.

Let v be the larger of the two valuations of p−1 and p+1 at 2. For large e, the real-tower degree is 2e+2−v. It eventually absorbs the power of two in every other order. If t is the odd part of the order of ψ(p), the residue degree is then 2e+2−vt. Divide the field degree 2e+2h by that degree to get exactly 2vh/t primes.
