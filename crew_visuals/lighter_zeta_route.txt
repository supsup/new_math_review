What a lighter zeta proof would have to supply

Lean can check the analytic finish using 31 files of the archive. Its
one input is a bound on a weighted sum of primes and prime powers. This
puts the outstanding mathematics into a short, explicit statement.

The target

Give each prime power n = p^k the weight Λ(n) = log p and every other
integer weight zero. Define the corrected sum for x ≥ 1 by

    G(x) = sum over 1 ≤ n ≤ x of Λ(n)(x/n − 1)
           − x log x + (1 + γ)x,

where γ is Euler's constant. The retained hypothesis is:

    For every θ strictly between 1/2 and 1 and every cutoff Y₀,
    some Y ≥ Y₀ satisfies G(x) ≤ x^θ throughout [Y, Y^(3/2)].

The bound is one-sided, and the interval can change with θ and Y₀.
tools/rh_zeta_prime_bound.lean applies the archive's Landau criterion to
the single L-function ζ, then uses the functional equation to finish.

How the archive reaches the target

The earlier modulus-one variant still takes Green, the bundle, and the
weighted-length budget for the modulus-one character. The first cut asks
only for a tied family for that character at scale 3/2, for each sufficiently
small positive ε, with its windows and character data. In
tools/rh_mod_one_tied_windows.lean, Lean checks both that this suffices
for RH and that these same families supply the displayed prime-sum bound.
The two files expose an arithmetic construction followed by an analytic
finish. The prime-sum file checks the finish with the bound as an input.

Why the bound itself carries the hard part

There is a classical converse on paper. On RH, Schoenfeld's bound gives
E(t) = ψ(t) − t = O(√t log²t), with ψ(t) = sum of Λ(n) through t.
Partial summation gives the weighted sum in G(x) as
x log x + x times the integral of E(t)/t² from 1 to x. For real s > 1,
the integral of E(t)/t^(s+1) from 1 to infinity is
(−ζ′(s)/ζ(s))/s − 1/(s−1). The Laurent expansion at 1 and dominated
convergence give its limit −(1+γ). Consequently G(x) is −x times the
integral of E(t)/t² from x to infinity. The same estimate gives
|G(x)| = O(√x log²x), which supplies all sufficiently large intervals
for each θ in the display. Together with the checked implication this
makes the prime-window criterion classically equivalent to RH. This
converse is a paper calculation here.

A bounded look at the sum

tools/check_zeta_prime_window_prefix.py uses ball arithmetic to certify
G(x) < √x for every real x from 4 through 1,000,000. The first crossing
of that threshold is between 3.346873827339 and 3.346873827340. On each
[n,n+1], G(x)−√x is at most its chord plus 1/(8n), so checking the integer
endpoints covers the full continuum. This certificate covers a finite
prefix. The analytic input above asks for arbitrarily large intervals.
