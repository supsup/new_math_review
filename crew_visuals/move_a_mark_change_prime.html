    Why replacing one square adds two

    Expand the determinant along row 7. The numbers multiplying the entries in columns A through H are 10, −6, 3, 5, 4, −4, 1, −1. They depend only on the other seven rows. The 1s in A and E always contribute 10+4. The movable 1 adds 3 in C and 5 in D.

    Each determinant is prime. Reducing modulo that prime makes the determinant zero, while a seven-row minor of magnitude 1 keeps seven directions independent. Both boards use the fewest ones possible for their respective primes. Compare the mod-17 minimum with seven rows · Read the sparsity proof
