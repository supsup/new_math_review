Hunt for a zero off the line

This is the critical strip, shaded by the size of zeta (stretched sideways to fit). Dark wells show where zeta is small; white ticks mark the zeros computed on the line. Click a well for a true-shape close-up. Drag a box anywhere and the page counts the zeros inside it. Try to find one that is not on the dashed centre line.

What am I looking at?

The picture. Each point s of the strip (real part across, height up) is shaded by the size of zeta(s): black where it is zero, bright near the pole at s = 1. The strip is only 1 wide but 50 tall, so it is drawn stretched sideways, and each zero's well looks like a flat dash. The close-up is drawn at true shape and also shows the direction of zeta's value as colour: around a zero every colour appears once, with bright rings where the size is 1/2, 1/4 and 1/8.

Counting. To count the zeros inside a box, the page walks around the box's edge and watches the value of zeta turn. Each zero inside makes it turn around 0 once for each copy of the zero (a double zero twice), and the pole at s = 1 takes one turn away (the argument principle). This counts every zero in the box, on the line or not, without looking for them. If an edge passes through a zero or the pole, there is no clean count: the page says so and asks you to move the edge.

What you will find. Every zero up to height 100 sits on the line. The Riemann Hypothesis, proved in 2026 and checked by computer, says that this holds at every height. This page only computes; it does not prove anything, and its arithmetic is ordinary floating point.
