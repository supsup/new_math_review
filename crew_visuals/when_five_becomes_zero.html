When five becomes zero

Multiply each column by its weight and add across each row. The last row gives 5. Switch to arithmetic modulo 5 and watch that last sum become zero too.

The last sum is 5. The five columns are independent over the fractions.

Why this is a smallest example

The board has 13 ones and determinant 5. Its first four equations force every column weight to be a multiple of the last one, in the proportions 2, 2, −1, −1, 1. The last equation becomes five times the last weight equals zero. Over fractions it forces that weight to be zero. Modulo 5 it leaves one free choice, so the rank drops from five to four.

A smaller ordinary rank would give a nonzero determinant from a square subtable of size at most four. Any such determinant is at most 3 in absolute value, as the four-row scan checks. Smaller subtables can be padded to size four with identity entries. So a 5 × 5 board losing rank modulo 5 must have a nonzero determinant divisible by 5.
