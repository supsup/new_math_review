Eight connections recover seven missing readings

At prime 12,871,291, put 12,871,290 inputs on a circle. Each mixed reading
adds the eight inputs at these offsets from its own position.

    0, 120092, 567832, 2537342, 2624638, 3857764, 5427245, 6166315

The board is invertible over the rationals. Modulo the prime its rank is
12,871,283. Remove any seven mixed readings. The survivors, together with
any seven direct input observations, determine every original input.
Eight taps attain the lower bound for this guarantee. If a nonzero row
used at most seven inputs, its equation would restrict coordinates that
must vary freely in the kernel.

Why the straight exponential gives an extra root

The d-tap recipe based on E(t) = sum of t^j/j! through degree d gives zero
address moments at exponents 2 through d. For even d it gives another
zero at exponent 2d + 2. To see this, differentiate the logarithm.

    E'(t)/E(t) = 1 − t^d / (d! E(t)).

The coefficient of t^(d+1) in 1/E(t) is the coefficient in exp(−t),
plus 1/(d+1)! from the first omitted term of E. Those contributions
cancel for even d. Thus E'/E has zero coefficient at t^(2d+1).
Writing E as the product of (1 + x_i t) turns that coefficient into
minus the sum of x_i^(2d+2). When the prime exceeds 2d+3, the resulting
circulant on the full multiplicative group has this extra root in
addition to the roots we wanted. For eight taps the extra exponent is 18.

The shifted construction

Use the degree-eight truncation of exp(t − b t²/2), with b = 6,826,730.
The degree-nine coefficient is zero at this prime. The truncation splits
into factors (1 + x_i t) over the field, with these eight addresses.

    6251446, 7032005, 7394094, 9503192, 10513549, 11507449, 12319869, 12706143

The logarithm agrees with t − b t²/2 through degree nine, so all seven
power sums at exponents 3 through 9 vanish. Take logarithms to base 10
and rotate the least offset to zero. The replay checks every power of 10
to establish that the seven expected roots are the only ones. The right
kernel consists of sequences 1000^j P(10^j) for polynomials of degree at
most six. Any seven direct probes therefore determine the invisible
part of the inputs by interpolation. Reversing indices gives the left
kernel, so any relation among surviving mixed readings would have seven
zeros and would be identically zero.

To check ordinary invertibility, choose auxiliary prime 64,356,451 and
an element of order 12,871,290 there. For each divisor m of the circle
size, raise the element to a power that gives order m. The wiring
polynomial evaluates nonzero at each such element. If it shared a
rational factor with T^12,871,290 − 1, that factor would include some
cyclotomic polynomial Φ_m, so it would vanish at the checked element
of order m. One evaluation per divisor suffices for this rational check.

The cubic shift gives a shorter board

At prime 1,630,987, take the degree-eight truncation of
exp(t + 1049519 t² + 462134 t³). Its degree-nine and degree-ten
coefficients vanish. Those two equations are polynomials in the two
coefficients. Eliminating the cubic coefficient by a resultant gives a
degree-fifteen polynomial in the quadratic coefficient, which makes
searching primes for candidates practical. The replay checks the selected
coefficients directly.

The eight factor addresses and the rotated base-2 offsets are

    235911, 400580, 529184, 586582, 974843, 982688, 1261976, 1552185
    0, 109707, 401729, 466398, 694256, 807724, 875762, 935276

The only seven modular roots are 2^4 through 2^10. The kernel and recovery
argument above then use P(2^j) with multiplier 16^j. The board has
1,630,986 inputs and rank 1,630,979. Auxiliary prime 8,154,931 with root
16,807 certifies ordinary invertibility at all sixteen orders.
