Thirteen ways to get seventeen with 23 ones

The seven-row Fano puzzle needs 24 ones. Across all sizes, the fewest ones is 23, and all the boards that achieve it have eight effective rows and columns. There are 13 shapes once row and column order are ignored. If you also allow turning rows into columns, there are eight.

How the count was made

The eight-row search has 40 qualifying boards in its local lexicographic order. For each one, try every permutation of its columns and sort the resulting rows. The least form is the same for all boards related in this way. Count how many different arrays the column permutations give each form. All counted boards have 23 ones and determinant magnitude 17.

The board counts keep column labels fixed and ignore row order. There are 372,960 in total. The three self-transpose shapes and five transpose pairs give eight shapes with transposes identified.

Why bigger witnesses add only empty rows or columns

A nonzero minor of maximal ordinary rank must have determinant divisible by 17. The global support bound is 23, so a minimum witness uses every one of its ones in that minor. All rows or columns outside it are zero. Inside the minor every row and column must have at least two ones, since a single 1 would give a smaller witness by expansion. This limits the size to eleven. The seven-row spectrum covers sizes through seven. In sizes nine, ten and eleven the signed-component search at support 23 checks 16,621,768 smaller systems altogether and finds no determinant divisible by 17. Thus the only effective size at the support minimum is eight.
