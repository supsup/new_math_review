Other pairs from the mod-five dial

When a high quartet first turns negative

Take the certified zero at height 1,174,561 in the 135° mix of the two mod-five functions. In Li’s sum, its quartet first contributes a negative term at power 7,379,974. The first negative run lasts 23 powers.

A full turn brings the negative term into view

Send the upper-right zero ρ to w = (ρ−1)/ρ. Write its magnitude as e−κ and its angle as φ. Conjugation and reflection supply the other three values conj(w), 1/w, conj(1/w). Together they give

Qn = 4[1 − cosh(nκ) cos(nφ)].

The angle φ is about 0.851 millionths of a radian. Raising to a power takes millions of steps to make one full turn. The magnitude factor changes slowly, so at the first turn the negative window is only about twenty millionths of a radian wide.

Why no earlier power is negative

Negativity requires nφ to lie within d(n) = arctan(sinh(nκ)) of a multiple of 2π. Here φ > κ and d(n) < nκ, so the interval never reaches zero for positive powers. Both endpoints increase because d has derivative κ/cosh(nκ). The upper endpoint first passes 2π at 7,379,974. The lower endpoint stays beneath it through 7,379,996 and passes it at the next power.

Three powers already detect it in a matrix

Take the second differences dk = Qk+1 − 2Qk + Qk−1, with Q−k = Qk. Put d|i−j| in position i, j. The 1×1 and 2×2 matrices are positive definite. At size three the vector (1, −1.999999999999275149, 1) gives a negative quadratic form, about −1.56 × 10−47, from the first three positive terms.

Why the three-by-three test works

Write w + 1/w = a + bi and η = 2−a. Then d₀ = 4η and d₁ = 2ηa + 2b², with d₀−d₁ = 2(η²−b²). The replay verifies a > 0 and η > |b|, so both eigenvalues of the two-by-two matrix are positive.

In size three, put weights (1, −x, 1) and let δ = x−a. Expanding the form gives 4ηδ² − 8b²δ − 4ηb². Taking x close to a leaves the last term dominant. The replay uses the same exact decimal vector throughout the root enclosure.

The size-three test works for every single quartet from a nonreal w off the unit circle. Then b ≠ 0. When a < 2, choose x = a in the formula. When a = 2, the vector (1, −1, 0) works. When a > 2, the diagonal itself is negative. Read the Lean proof for a single quartet.
