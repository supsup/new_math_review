Six matrices and one cuspidal character

The compiled implication toward the Riemann Hypothesis starts with a statement called Green. Its smallest case with two rows has only six matrices. Each one shuffles three nonzero binary vectors, and that shuffle tells us which character meets the premise.

All entries are computed modulo 2. In particular, 1 + 1 = 0.

Which character survives the test?

A character records the trace of a matrix’s action in a representation. Here the trivial representation always acts by 1. The sign representation gives −1 for a swap and +1 for an even shuffle. The standard representation has character equal to the number of fixed points minus one.

In this case the archive calls an irreducible character cuspidal when the shear group leaves no nonzero vector fixed in its representation. On the sign representation the shear acts by −1. Over the complex numbers, only the zero vector equals its own negative.

One character, one label. The sign character gets T² + T + 1, the only monic quadratic with no root in the two-element field. Its dimension is 1, which matches Green’s formula here, 2 − 1.

How the six matrices give the whole answer

The images of the two coordinate vectors can be any two distinct nonzero vectors, giving 3 × 2 = 6 invertible matrices. They produce all six permutations. The three characters in the table are irreducible and exhaust the possibilities. Their inner products, averaged over the six matrices, give three unit norms and zero off the diagonal. Their squared dimensions add to 1 + 1 + 4 = 6.

The shear group has two elements, the identity and the matrix that fixes (1,0) and swaps (0,1) with (1,1). Averaging those two actions projects onto their fixed vectors, so the last column is (trace at identity + trace at shear) / 2. The same calculation applies to the conjugate shear groups that fix each of the other two lines.

The archive also gives a trace formula on matrices with irreducible characteristic polynomial. Here they are the two 3-cycles. Their eigenvalues in the field of four elements are the two nonidentity cube roots. The regular character in the formula sends them to the complex numbers ω and ω², so Green’s trace formula is −(ω + ω²) = 1. This agrees with the sign of a 3-cycle.
