Ordinary class groups for every odd coefficient prime
=====================================================

The odd-prime cutoff note gives an exact ordinary class-group
description of each finite torsion layer when ℓ−1 does not divide
the twist j. When ℓ−1 divides j, local fixed points introduce
an error. For an unramified odd coefficient prime, that error
in module length is at most 2[K:ℚ](1+v_ℓ(j)), uniformly over all
torsion layers. It fits into the window budget after summation.
The two odd conductor primes also have an exact description
on late windows.

Setup
-----

Let K be the CM host, ℓ an odd rational prime unramified in K,
and j ≥ 1 with ℓ−1 dividing j. Let A = ℚ_ℓ/ℤ_ℓ(−j) and
B = A[ℓ^a] for a ≥ 1. Put E = K(μ_(ℓ^a)), Δ = Gal(E/K),
h = 1+v_ℓ(j) and d = min(a,h). Write s for the number of places
of K over ℓ. Their cyclotomic groups are all of Δ, since each
K_v is unramified and the cyclotomic extension is totally
ramified. Write T(K,−) for the strict kernel defined in
zeta_budget_odd_parts.txt.

Let W_a = Hom_Δ(Cl(E),B), with the ordinary class group of E.
Write W_a^0 for those maps that kill the primes over ℓ.
The comparison is

    | length(T(K,A)[ℓ^a]) − length(W_a) | ≤ 2s d.

The same bound holds on the archive's Plus and Minus parts.
All lengths in this display are over ℤ_ℓ.

Passing from A to finite coefficients
-------------------------------------

The global fixed submodule C = A^(G_K) has order ℓ^h, and all
its local counterparts at ℓ are the same C. Indeed the tame
part acts trivially, and the cyclotomic unit 1+ℓ has
v_ℓ((1+ℓ)^j−1) = h. The rest of the wild part is generated
topologically by this unit.

The cohomology sequence for multiplication by ℓ^a on A
names C/ℓ^a C as the kernel when passing from H¹ with
coefficients B to the ℓ^a-torsion in H¹ with coefficients A.
This holds globally and in each place over ℓ. Away from
ℓ the injection on H¹ of inertia still holds, since its
fixed module is the divisible group A.

A lift of a strict class in T(K,A)[ℓ^a] is therefore already
unramified away from ℓ. Its restrictions at the s places
over ℓ belong to the local copies of C/ℓ^a C. Changing the
lift adds the same element in each copy. Reducing modulo
this diagonal makes the tuple independent of the lift.
The tuple is zero exactly when a strict lift exists. The
global kernel C/ℓ^a C injects into each local copy, so the
strict lift, when it exists, is unique. Thus T(K,B) injects
into T(K,A)[ℓ^a], with cokernel of length at most (s−1)d.

Passing from finite coefficients to the class group
---------------------------------------------------

Restriction into E sends the strict finite kernel into W_a^0.
It is injective there. An inflated H¹(Δ,B) class that vanishes
locally at any v over ℓ must be zero, since the local
cyclotomic quotient is all of Δ.

Conversely, every map in W_a^0 admits a global H¹ lift.
The transgression in H²(Δ,B) restricts to zero at a place
over ℓ, because the map kills the local absolute group
upstairs by reciprocity. At that place the quotient is
all of Δ and the restriction map on the transgression
target is the identity. Its global obstruction is zero.

The lift is unramified away from ℓ since inertia is unchanged
in E/K there. Its local values at ℓ are uniquely the
inflations of a tuple in H¹(Δ,B)^s. Changing the global lift
changes the tuple by a diagonal element of H¹(Δ,B). This
again gives a well-defined obstruction, with kernel exactly
T(K,B).

Here H¹(Δ,B) has length d−1. To compute it, average away
the tame subgroup and use the cyclic wild group of order
ℓ^(a−1), with a generator acting by u = (1+ℓ)^(−j) on B.
The cyclic cohomology quotient is the kernel of the norm
operator divided by (u−1)B. The norm has valuation a−1 by
the lifting-the-exponent identity, so its kernel in B is
ℓB. The valuation of u−1 is h. The resulting length is d−1,
including the trivial wild group at a=1.

Consequently W_a^0/T(K,B) has length at most (s−1)(d−1).
Allowing the primes over ℓ back in adds at most sd, by
evaluating at their s fixed ideal classes and landing in
(B^Δ)^s. Combining the two comparisons to T(K,B) gives the
stated absolute error bound. The maps respect the action of
conjugation of K, which permutes the places over ℓ. The two
sign projectors at odd ℓ are exact, so the same inequalities
apply to each sign. On W_a those signs again come from the
real and relative class groups of E.

What this buys at the window scale
----------------------------------

Every class in T(K,A) is killed by some ℓ^a, since a continuous
cocycle into the discrete torsion module has finite image.
The length of the full group is the supremum of the lengths
of these finite layers. One may therefore use the supremum
of the corresponding class-group lengths with the same
additive error. No transition maps on the class groups are
needed to make the numerical comparison.

If ℓ−1 divides j and 1 ≤ j < t then ℓ ≤ t. The weighted error
per line is at most

    2s h log ℓ ≤ 2[K:ℚ](log ℓ + log j) ≤ 4[K:ℚ] log t.

There are at most t² such lines, so the total error is at
most 4[K:ℚ] t² log t. In a modulus-one window this grows
polynomially in x and fits into the exponential allowance.
For all other unramified twists the earlier class-group
description is exact.

Thus the unramified odd portion of the residual budget can
be bounded using ordinary class groups for every twist.

The two conductor primes
------------------------

For the modulus-one host K = R(√−q,√−d), the only odd
ramified primes are q and d. Let ℓ be one of them. The
congruences q ≡ 3 mod 8 and d ≡ 7 mod 16 imply ℓ ≡ 3 mod 4.
The local ramified factor is ℚ_ℓ(√−ℓ), the quadratic subfield
of ℚ_ℓ(μ_ℓ). The tower R and the other quadratic factor are
unramified at ℓ. Thus K_v is the compositum of an unramified
field and ℚ_ℓ(√−ℓ).

The intersection of K_v with ℚ_ℓ(μ_(ℓ^a)) is exactly that
quadratic subfield, since all subfields of the cyclotomic
field are totally ramified and K_v has ramification degree
two. Globally K already contains ℚ(√−ℓ), so the local and
global relative cyclotomic groups again coincide at each
place over ℓ. Each such place has a unique prime above it
in E. The same local argument now gives the exact ordinary
class-group description if (ℓ−1)/2 does not divide j. This
is the order of the tame cyclotomic subgroup over K.

This condition holds for all twists 1 ≤ j < t on sufficiently
late selected windows. The lower bounds on log q and log d in
WinPair, with Y ≥ x^(1/J) and fixed ε,J, grow at least as
x^(ε/(5J))/2. Hence both primes eventually exceed 2t+1 when
t = floor(x^400).

For the sign comparison at these primes use full complex
conjugation on E, which restricts to conjugation on K. It
acts on the coefficient B by (−1)^j. Accordingly the
archive's Plus eigenvalue (−1)^j means that the class-group
map is even for full conjugation, and the Minus eigenvalue
means it is odd. Norm identifies the even part with the
ℓ-primary class group of the real subfield as before.

The entire odd residual budget can therefore be expressed
using ordinary real and relative class groups, with the
polynomial error on the unramified tame-trivial lines.
Uniform bounds on the selected components remain the
arithmetic input.

Scope
-----

This is a paper comparison using the same restriction
sequences and class field theory as zeta_odd_prime_cutoff.txt.
In a finite arithmetic check, the formula for the cyclic
H¹ quotient agreed in 367 cases at ℓ = 3, 5, 7 and 11. The
check used all positive multiples of ℓ−1 below 100 and layers
through a = 4 at 3 and 5, or a = 3 at 7 and 11.
