A little gap to the right

Suppose a zero sat just to the right of ½. You would only need a prime-power bound with an exponent slightly closer to ½ than that zero is. Lean puts an intermediate line θ in the gap, then chooses a small enough ε to meet the bound on that line.

The toy uses the estimate exponent ½ + 12ε. For β between ½ and 1, Lean puts θ halfway from ½ to β. The toy chooses ε = (θ − ½)/24, with room to spare. In the proof, the coefficient of ε comes from the family, and ε must also be below that family’s cutoff.

  1. Fit a window bound under θ.

    level_of_tiedWin makes the choice of ε and uses the family bound on arbitrarily large windows. It passes FormBAt the resulting inequality Gm(y) ≤ yθ. The choice in Lean

  2. Give that bound to the analytic criterion.

    Comparison₂.formB rewrites the weighted statistic as a positive combination for a family of characters. zeroFree_of_rpow_window turns the pointwise bound into an area budget and calls lemmaL. The family conversion · Try the area budget

  3. Rule out a pole past θ.

    lemmaL builds the transform and applies landau_window. A zero past θ would give a pole whose contribution grows too quickly, relative to the bound, as the Taylor order rises. The hypothetical zero at β lies in the region just ruled out. The call to Landau’s criterion · See the growth comparison
