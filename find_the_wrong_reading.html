Why every single error has a unique address

The 12-input mixer has 48 connections and ordinary determinant −121. Modulo 11 it loses two dimensions. Two independent weighted sums of its mixed readings always give zero. Together the sums are called a syndrome.

Each reading contributes a different direction to the syndrome. Changing a single reading by a nonzero amount moves along its direction. There are 12 choices of location and 10 nonzero amounts, so the 120 single mistakes fill all 120 nonzero points of the grid. Each point therefore tells the decoder where to subtract an amount.

Two altered values also give a nonzero syndrome. It still points to one reading, so a decoder that assumes one error can force the checks to pass while returning the wrong vector. Try two changes to see this happen. Recovering the original input values takes two direct input readings in addition to the corrected mix. See the direct-input puzzle
