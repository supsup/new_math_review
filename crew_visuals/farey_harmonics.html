What the Farey circle adds up to

Turn a fraction into an arrow around the unit circle. Add the arrows for every reduced denominator up to N. Their ends look complicated, yet the horizontal sum is exactly an integer. A divisibility matrix gives the same number through its determinant.

Start with the first turn

To draw the arrow for a/q, make k × a/q turns. Conjugate arrows cancel vertically. When k = 1, all the arrows with denominator q sum to the Möbius number μ(q). That number is zero if q has a repeated prime factor, and otherwise it is +1 or −1 according to whether the number of prime factors is even or odd. The entire circle sum is their running total M(N), the same first harmonic Landau singled out in 1924. Landau, 1924, p. 202.

In the mod-five view every denominator q gets the weight χ(q). The weight is +1 at remainders 1 and 4, −1 at 2 and 3, and zero at 0. A −1 reverses the arrow. A zero removes it. The first harmonic now sums μ(q)χ(q), giving the twisted Mertens function Mχ(N).

Try N = 15 and k = 13 with every fraction counted. All the arrows from denominator 13 line up. Since 13 is prime, the total is M(15) + 13 = 12. Lower N to 12 and that entire contribution disappears. This is why the growth statement below fixes the harmonic.

Why more turns still give an integer

Among all q roots on the circle, the sum after k turns is q if q divides k, and zero otherwise. Split those roots by their reduced denominators and invert the divisor sum. The contribution from denominator q is the Ramanujan sum ∑d | gcd(q,k) d μ(q/d). Regrouping over all q up to N gives the exact circle sum ∑d | k, d ≤ N dχ(d) Mχ(⌊N/d⌋). In the first view, take χ identically 1. Ramanujan, 1918, §1.

Why the determinant is the same integer

At row i and column j after the first column, write χ(j/i) when i divides j and zero otherwise. In the first column write the sum of dχ(d) over the divisors d of k with di ≤ N. With one turn and weights 1, the first column is all ones and this is the Redheffer matrix.

Combine the rows with coefficients μ(i)χ(i) and put the result in the first row. Its original coefficient is one, so the determinant stays the same. Every column j > 1 cancels because each term factors as χ(j) times μ(i) and the Möbius numbers of the divisors of j sum to zero. The remaining minor is triangular with diagonal ones. Its determinant is one, leaving only the upper-left number, exactly the circle sum above.

How a larger board uses only 0 and 1

Use the 0 and 1 button on the matrix above. The new block at the bottom right supplies signed powers of two. A row that needs +5 in the first column can select −1 and −4. Eliminating the block subtracts their sum and restores +5. The determinant stays the same.

First give row and column i the same sign, χ of i with all factors of 5 removed. Multiplying by those signs leaves the determinant unchanged. When i divides j and j/i is coprime to 5, its old sign is the ratio of the two labels, so the new entry is 1. A quotient divisible by 5 already has entry zero. In the unweighted view all label signs are +1.

Leave the first column of the old board empty. The new triangular block T has diagonal ones and only connects to earlier rows. The one at the first helper row of the first column makes the top helper value +1 in the solution of Th = (1, 0, …, 0). Each other helper value is the negative of the sum of the earlier values its row selects. From +1 make −1, then another +1. Use both +1s to make −2, then flip it to +2. The positive values so far sum to 4, so the next pair is −4 and +4. Continue to the largest bit needed.

For each old row, select the signed powers of two that sum to the negative of its required first entry. The elimination subtracts that sum from the first entry and leaves every other old entry alone. Since T has determinant 1, the full 0 and 1 board has the original determinant. The exact replay checks both boards for every setting in the explorer. Exact replay.

What the Riemann hypotheses say about the integers

For each fixed character, Dirichlet GRH bounds the twisted first harmonic by Oχ,ε(N1/2 + ε) for every ε > 0. The bound also implies the relevant zero-free half-plane because 1/L(s,χ) = s∫1∞Mχ(x)x−s−1 dx continues there. The sum over divisors shows the same exponent for every fixed harmonic k. The untwisted case uses ordinary RH. The mod-five case uses the archive’s Dirichlet statement for the quadratic character. The finite identities hold unconditionally. Halupczok and Suger, partial Möbius sums under GRH · OpenAI, The Quasi-Riemann Hypothesis, Möbius-sum reduction · Archive statements.

Marlow noticed the converse works at any one fixed k. The Dirichlet series of the contributions as denominators enter has the form S(s)/L(s,χ), where S(s) = ∑d | k χ(d)d1−s. For a prime power pa in k, the corresponding factor of S is 1 + u + … + ua, with u = χ(p)p1−s. All its roots have |u| = 1, so any zeros of S lie on Re s = 1. In the open strip 1/2 < Re s < 1, the numerator cannot cancel a zero of L. Thus the same growth bound at any single fixed harmonic implies zero-freeness in that strip and, by the functional equation, the corresponding hypothesis.
