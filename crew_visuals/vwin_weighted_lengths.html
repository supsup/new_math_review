One budget across primes

Another input to the compiled RH implication is called VWin. It puts one budget on a collection of groups indexed by prime and level. A layer at prime 5 uses more of that budget than a layer at prime 2.

The same total in two forms

A finite-length module over the p-adic integers has simple layers of size p. Its length counts the layers. Two layers at 3 give 3² = 9 elements, whether the module looks like or like two copies of ℤ/3ℤ. Taking the logarithm gives a cost of 2 log 3 in either case.

Across levels, add the lengths for a fixed prime and multiply by log p. Across primes, add again. The result is exactly the logarithm of the product of the sizes. The toy uses only three primes and two levels so you can see each contribution.

The third input asks for a single record of compatible choices and conditions. See how the archive fits those pieces together.

Where the archive’s statement uses the budget

VVle first asks for each length to be finite, for levels 1 ≤ e < t. It then bounds the weighted sum for every finite set of primes by the same number B. The lengths belong to submodules called Sha in the Galois-cohomology setup.

One prime alone gives a consequence. A nonzero length at p contributes at least log p, so the singleton set would already exceed the budget if p > exp(B). In particular only finitely many primes can contribute at all.

VWin calls this bound for a range of scales in certain number fields. At a scale x, it sets the level cutoff to ⌊x401⌋ and the budget to √exp(xε). The group construction and the choice of scales are part of the archive’s full premise.
