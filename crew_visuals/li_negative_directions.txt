How many directions can an off-line quartet spoil?
==================================================

The three-by-three test in li_cousin_quartet.html sees a single quartet on
its own. The masking experiment mixes one such quartet with the true ζ
coefficients, and its first 24 matrix tests can all pass. What happens if
we let the matrix grow as large as needed?

For the full ζ Li second-difference matrices, each distinct off-line
quartet eventually accounts for two negative eigenvalues. If there are
exactly Q distinct off-line quartets, the number of negative eigenvalues
is exactly 2Q for every sufficiently large size. If there are infinitely
many, that number grows without bound. The count uses distinct quartets
and multiplicities enter the form as positive weights.

A negative eigenvalue means that some real choice of weights makes the
matrix's quadratic form negative. Two negative eigenvalues give a plane
of choices on which every nonzero choice makes the form negative.

The calculation
---------------

Write w = (ρ−1)/ρ for a zero. The zero symmetries turn into conjugation
and inversion of w. For an off-line quartet choose the outer node
w with |w| > 1. Its nodes are w, conj(w), 1/w, conj(1/w). These
are distinct since the ζ zeros in the open strip are nonreal.
For real s in (0,1) the alternating zeta series is positive and
ζ(s) is that series divided by 1−2^(1−s), so ζ(s) < 0. Set
c = 2 − w − 1/w. Write m for the positive multiplicity of each of its
zeros.

The Li second-difference matrix has diagonal 2λ₁ and entry
λ_{k+1} − 2λ_k + λ_{k−1} at distance k ≥ 1 from the diagonal.
Here λ₀ = 0. A real vector in the matrix of size N can be read as
the coefficients of a real polynomial p of degree < N. The quartet
contributes the following to that vector's quadratic form.

    4m Re(c p(w) p(1/w))

This follows by taking the second difference of
4 − 2 Re(wⁿ + w⁻ⁿ). For an on-line pair, |w| = 1 and 1/w = conj(w).
The corresponding contribution is 2m c |p(w)|², with
c = 2 − 2 Re(w) ≥ 0.

If there are Q off-line quartets, the form can have at most 2Q negative
directions at any size. Indeed, impose the Q complex linear evaluations
p(w) = 0 on the real coefficient vectors. The surviving subspace has
real codimension at most 2Q. Each off-line contribution vanishes there,
and the sum of the on-line contributions is nonnegative.

To get the lower bound, select any Q distinct off-line quartets and let
R > 1 be the smallest outer modulus of the selected nodes. Collect all
the off-line quartets with outer modulus at least R.
There are only finitely many, because w tends to 1 as the zeros tend to
infinite height. Every remaining exterior modulus is at most R₁ for
some 1 ≤ R₁ < R. For example, there are finitely many outer moduli
above (1+R)/2, and the remaining ones below R have a maximum below R.

Choose a complex parameter α_j per selected quartet. For each integer
k ≥ 0 use real polynomial interpolation to obtain A and B_k on all
the collected nodes. Use the following values at the selected nodes.

    A(1/w_j) = α_j              B_k(1/w_j) = 0
    A(w_j)   = 0                B_k(w_j) = −conj(c_j α_j) (w_j/|w_j|)^(−k)

Use conjugate values at conjugate nodes and set both polynomials to
zero at the nodes of the unselected collected quartets. Standard
Lagrange interpolation on the finite set gives real coefficients
because the node and value lists respect conjugation. The degrees of
A and B_k are bounded by a constant independent of k. Their
coefficients are bounded by a constant times ||α||, the Euclidean
norm of the Q complex parameters.

Now put p_k(z) = A(z) + z^k B_k(z). At each selected inner node its
value is α_j. At each selected outer node its value is
−conj(c_j α_j) |w_j|^k. Thus the selected quartets contribute

    −4 ∑_j m_j |c_j|² |α_j|² |w_j|^k ≤ −C R^k ||α||²

for some C > 0. The unselected collected quartets contribute zero.
For every remaining pair or quartet, the product p_k(z) p_k(1/z)
contains powers 1, z^k and z^(−k). Both z and 1/z lie in the fixed
annulus 1/R₁ ≤ |z| ≤ R₁, so the product is bounded in absolute value
by C₁ R₁^k ||α||² for a constant C₁ independent of k and the node.

The sum of m |c| over all pairs and quartets is finite.
In terms of ρ, c = −1/(ρ(ρ−1)). Its absolute value is of order
1/|ρ|² at large height, and the ζ zero count through height T is
O(T log T). This also justifies summing the second differences
absolutely for each fixed entry. Therefore the total contribution of
the remaining roots is bounded by C₂ R₁^k ||α||². The selected negative
term wins for all sufficiently large k since R₁ < R.

The map from the Q complex parameters to p_k is real-linear and
injective because p_k(1/w_j) = α_j. This yields a 2Q-dimensional
negative subspace, in size at most k plus a fixed constant. Increasing
the matrix size preserves it by padding coefficient vectors with
zeros. This proves the eventual lower bound for every finite choice
of Q. The upper bound above completes the finite count.

How fast the negative eigenvalues grow in magnitude
---------------------------------------------------

List the outer moduli of the distinct off-line quartets in decreasing
order, keeping ties between different quartets. Write r_q for the qth
modulus. Let μ_j(N) be the jth smallest eigenvalue of the size-N
matrix. If q = ceil(j/2) and there are at least q quartets, then

    lim_{N → ∞} (−μ_j(N))^(1/N) = r_q.

For the lower bound on −μ_j(N), choose the first q quartets above
and let R = r_q. The coefficient norm of p_k is at most D ||α||
for a constant D, because multiplying by z^k only shifts the
coefficients of B_k. The negative quadratic bound therefore gives
a Rayleigh quotient at most −C₃ R^k on the whole 2q-dimensional
subspace for all large k. Take k = N−1−d with d a fixed bound on the
interpolation degrees. The minimum-maximum principle then gives
−μ_j(N) ≥ C₄ R^N.

For the upper bound, impose p(w) = 0 at the outer nodes of the s
quartets with modulus strictly greater than R. This costs at most
2s < j real dimensions. Every remaining off-line quartet has
|w| ≤ R, so Cauchy–Schwarz bounds |p(w)p(1/w)| by
N R^(N−1) times the squared coefficient norm. The on-line terms
are nonnegative and the weights m |c| are summable. Hence the
Rayleigh quotient on this subspace is at least −C₅ N R^(N−1).
The same minimum-maximum principle bounds −μ_j(N) from above
by C₅ N R^(N−1). Taking Nth roots gives the claimed rate.

An exact small example
----------------------

Take w = 2i, with multiplicity one, and an on-line pair at i, −i
with weight 100. The polynomials

    A(z) = (16 + 4z²)/15
    B(z) = (z² + 1/4)(8/15 + z/5)

give A(−i/2) = 1, A(2i) = 0, B(−i/2) = 0 and
B(2i) = −conj(2 − 3i/2). Use p_k(z) = A(z) + z^k B(z) for k a
multiple of four. The on-line pair contributes 73 each time because
i^k = 1. The off-line quartet contributes −25·2^k. For k = 0 the
sum on this vector is 48. For k = 4 it is −327.

Relation to the literature
--------------------------

The Li zero sum and its convention come from Li's criterion.
Xian-Jin Li, The positivity of a sequence of numbers and the
Riemann hypothesis. https://doi.org/10.1006/jnth.1997.2137

The identity (1−w)(1−1/w) = c writes the matrix form as Weil's
zero-side quadratic form on functions (1−w)p(w). Its basis functions
(1−w)w^j are differences of the test polynomials 1−w^j used in §4 of
Enrico Bombieri and Jeffrey C. Lagarias, Complements to Li's criterion
for the Riemann hypothesis. https://websites.umich.edu/~lagarias/doc/bombieri.pdf

Bombieri obtains the analogous negative-eigenvalue count for finite
Fourier truncations of Weil's quadratic functional. The construction
above gives polynomial witnesses for the Li second-difference
truncations. Enrico Bombieri, Remarks on Weil's quadratic functional
in the theory of prime numbers, I, Theorems 8–9.
https://www.bdim.eu/item?fmt=pdf&id=RLIN_2000_9_11_3_183_0

Anthropic's E2 transcript gives a related full Weil-form argument using
Gaussian times polynomial test functions and makes the distinct-zero
qualification explicit. See its Theorem 1, summarized on PDF page 20.
https://assets.claude.com/8a0d1add3c637b858a9a181e98c40e9548c3f44f.pdf#page=20
