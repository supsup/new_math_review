Why one estimate covers every angle

Normalize the coefficient of 1 to be 1. The coefficients repeat every five integers, with values 1, a(2), −a(2), −1, 0. Let c be the real part of the root number. Then

|a(2)|² = (1 − c sin(2τ)) / (1 + c sin(2τ)) ≤ (1 + c) / (1 − c) = κ−2.

The Gauss sum gives c² = (5 + √5)/10. Using √5 < 2.2361 gives κ = √((1 − c)/(1 + c)) > 71/250 by a rational comparison. Thus every coefficient on residues ±2 has magnitude less than 250/71, uniformly in τ.

At real part 5/2, bound each displayed term n−5/2 by replacing √n in the denominator by a lower rational bound. Beyond a square cutoff N, the sum of these terms is at most the integral from N to infinity, or 2/(3N3/2). Multiply the remainder by 250/71. The terms through 16 plus the remainder give a rational upper bound below 1.

How Lean checks the entire wall

Choose “15 · Lean proof” to see a slightly smaller bound. The Lean file computes the character and its Gauss sum, checks the coefficient limits, and proves that the two analytic L-functions cannot cancel for any real mixing angle at Re s ≥ 5/2.

After term 15, group the terms into blocks of five. Each block has one term of weight zero, two of weight 1, and two with weight at most R = 250/71. For a block starting at x, √n ≥ 4 and n ≥ x. The bound per unit of weight is 1/(4x²), below 1/((2x−5)(2x+5)).

That last fraction is (1/(2x−5) − 1/(2x+5))/10. The negative term cancels the first term for the next block, leaving at most (2 + 2R)/270 for all the blocks together. The displayed bound, 0.9942, is below the leading 1.
