Multiplicity

Why the pitch drops

At a simple zero, halving the distance cuts the ideal value in half. At a double zero it cuts it to a quarter, and at a triple zero to an eighth. We turn those ratios into pitch. Each factor of two drops the pitch one octave.

The asymmetric curve multiplies the ideal shape by a smooth factor that tends to 1 near the root. Zoom in and the measured interval approaches the whole number of octaves again.

These shapes show what multiplicity means near a root. In the zeta card, one location can contribute more than one zero to the total count. The zeta23 formalization counts simple zeros separately.
