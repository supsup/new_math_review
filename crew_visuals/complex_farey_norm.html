When complex Farey arrows give an integer determinant

Weight the fractions by 1, −1, i, −i or zero. The sum lands on a point a + bi with integer coordinates. A matrix with twice as many rows has determinant a² + b², the squared length of that arrow.

Rotate by a quarter turn

Choose the character mod 5 with χ(2) = i. On remainders 1, 2, 3, 4, 0 it has values 1, i, −i, −1, 0. The fractions with any fixed denominator give an integer Ramanujan sum before this weight is applied. Multiplication by i turns that integer contribution through 90 degrees. So the cumulative sum follows horizontal and vertical steps on the integer grid. The real-weight explorer and Ramanujan sum.

With N = 7 and one turn, the sum is 2 − i. Its conjugate is 2 + i and their product is 5. In this example the larger integer board has 14 rows.

Why the large integer board gives the same result

Build the weighted divisibility matrix from the circle page, using this χ. After the first column, position (i,j) is χ(j/i) when i divides j. The first column at row i sums dχ(d) over divisors d of k with di ≤ N. The same row operation gives the complex Farey sum as the determinant.

Replace each entry u + vi by the block with rows (u, −v) and (v, u). All these blocks act by u + vi on the vector (1, −i) and by u − vi on (1, i). Use this basis in every coordinate. The large board separates into the old board and its conjugate, so the determinant is (a + bi)(a − bi) = a² + b². Exact circle sums and integer determinants.

Use only zeros and ones in the large board

Give each index a unit label by applying χ after stripping off every factor of 5. Multiply row i by its label and column i by the inverse. The determinant stays the same. In columns after the first, each nonzero entry has a quotient coprime to 5, and its two labels cancel its character value. Thus every one of those Gaussian entries is 0 or 1.

Expanding the Gaussian entries to blocks leaves two integer columns to encode. Apply the signed powers-of-two construction to each with its own helper rows and columns. The extra size depends only on the largest coordinate in these two columns. For any fixed harmonic that coordinate is bounded by the sum of the divisors of k, independently of N. With N = 7 and k = 1, four helper rows suffice. The resulting 18 × 18 board still has determinant 5. The signed powers-of-two construction.

What the growth bound would say

The square root of the integer determinant is exactly the magnitude of the twisted harmonic. For any fixed turn count k, the fixed-character GRH growth criterion on the circle page can therefore be written as an integer determinant bound Oχ,k,ε(N1+ε) for every ε > 0.
